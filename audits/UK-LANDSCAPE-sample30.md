## 6 · The divergences that matter

**Underworld — #1 consumption, #12 UK audience.** 4.71M consumption,
the largest in the entire 145-string dataset, against 375,016 UK
monthly views and a 124.9M-lifetime channel. *Born Slippy (Nuxx)* is
26.4% of his consumption. This is catalogue moving through the pipe,
not an artist with a large current UK YouTube audience. Both facts are
true; only one of them is visible today.

**D-Block Europe — aligned on every lens.** #2 consumption, #1 UK
audience, UK is their #1 territory at 67.5%, +2.4pp above their own
median. Unambiguously major UK YouTube repertoire, and the one artist
here no lens disagrees about.

**Dave — the roll-up is vindicated by an independent source.** His solo
string ranks 9th at 1,257,602. His Chartmetric UK audience is
**5,376,965 — second-largest in the sample** — and the UK is his #1
territory. The analytical roll-up (4,317,431, indicative rank 2) lines
up with the external measurement; the solo row does not. Chartmetric
measures the artist, the consumption string measures one credit. This
is evidence the roll-up is the truer artist-level picture, arrived at
from a completely separate dataset.

**Morgan Wallen — huge globally, thin and softening in the UK.** 6.16bn
lifetime views, 62.9M views in 7 days (the largest channel activity in
the sample), but the UK is **1.8% of returned territories** and falling
at −9.1pp against his own median.

**aespa — no GB row at all.** 23 territories returned, none of them the
UK, against 6.75bn lifetime views and 52M weekly views. Recorded as
`inReturnedTerritories: false`, never as zero. Exactly the case the
sample was designed to catch.

**beabadoobee — the clearest live UK signal.** +36.7% against a +11.5%
median. Her UK is accelerating three times faster than her other
markets, on a 2.14M-subscriber channel with 14.7M weekly views.

**Bloc Party — over-indexing on the UK.** 107k subscribers, the second
smallest in Group B, but UK is #2 at 17.1% and **+9.5pp above their own
median**. Small channel, disproportionate and growing UK footprint.

**Luvcat — sharpest decline anywhere.** UK fell 316,145 → 138,754
(−56.1%) while her other territories rose (+7.3% median): −63.4pp, UK
dropped to #12. Yet her channel did 2.35M views in 7 days on 113k
subscribers. A UK moment that has passed while the channel stays busy —
worth a human look.

**The Long Faces — the Topic-channel anomaly.** 1.45M consumption
(rank 6), 4.2M views in 7 days on a 46k-subscriber channel — and **not
in Chartmetric at all**. An auto-generated Topic channel has no artist
entity behind it. The consumption is real; there is no owned surface
and no artist-level UK measurement. Channel metrics here are not the
same object as everywhere else in the table.

## 7 · Data quality issues

**Digga — excluded.** Chartmetric returned **3 territories** and a UK
figure of **67 monthly views**, for a 675k-subscriber channel with 142M
lifetime views and 163,642 26W consumption. 67 is not a plausible
monthly UK figure. `previousDate` came back **null** — the trend
consensus check could not pin a comparison date — so the "+34%" is 67
vs 50 and means nothing. The pipeline surfaced this rather than hiding
it, which is what the consensus rule was for. **Do not report Digga's
UK figures.**

**Reading dates are not uniform.** Two clusters: 21 Sep and 24 Sep.
Freshness must be stated per artist, never once for the table.

**Seven artists returned `no-territory-data`** — Chartmetric resolved
them but held no YouTube territory rows: Marnz Malone, Jamie Webster,
Fontaines D.C., Fredo, Original Koffee, LAZARO, Architects. Notable
that Fontaines D.C. (279k subs, 161M lifetime, a real UK band with
582,121 consumption) has none.

**Territory counts vary widely** — 3 to 26. Share-of-returned is
therefore not comparable across artists with very different counts, and
is correctly the secondary measure.

**No GB row is not zero.** aespa is recorded as absent, not as 0.

## 9 · Does this justify running all 76?

**Yes, with one qualification.**

What the sample proved: the join is deterministic and reliable (30/30
resolved to a channel ID, 22/30 to Chartmetric territory data); the
three lenses genuinely disagree, so they are not redundant; UK
territory rank is a real new axis; relative momentum produces a usable
three-band classification; and the blind-spot finding alone — eight
Virgin UK artists with real UK audiences invisible to consumption
reporting — would justify the exercise on its own.

The qualification: expect **~30% to return no territory data**, and
expect the misses to be skewed toward smaller and developing artists.
On the remaining 46 that means roughly 32 usable rows. Still worth
~92 requests and 90 seconds.

**But I would do the Daily Trend capture first.** See below.

## What the existing extraction proves about the format

`raw_daily.csv` (20,197 rows) is the product of these reports, and its
structure answers several questions definitively:

```
Date, Artist, Track, ISRC, Label, Supply Chain,
UK YouTube Consumption, Source Report
```

**The most important finding, and it contradicts the brief's
assumption.** Each report does **not** carry a single day:

- 26 distinct `Source Report` values cover **182 distinct dates**
- 24 of those reports carry exactly **7 dates**, one carries 4, one 10
- Lag between report date and consumption date runs **3 to 9 days**,
  each value appearing ~25 times

**So each Daily Trend Report contains a rolling 7-day window ending
three days before the report date.** Consecutive daily reports overlap
by six days. The 26W dataset was built by taking one report per week to
tile the period without overlap.

Three consequences for any pipeline:

1. **Naive daily ingestion produces ~7× duplication.** Deduplication on
   `Date + ISRC` is mandatory, not an optimisation.
2. **A missed day costs nothing.** Any single date appears in seven
   consecutive reports, so the next six can recover it. That is
   unusually forgiving.
3. **Consumption is always at least 3 days old.** "Yesterday" is never
   available. Any rolling window must be labelled against the
   consumption date, not the report date.

Also established from the data: **no platform column and no territory
column**. Platform and territory are implied by which tab you read —
this is the YouTube tab of a UK report. The desired schema's
`platform` and `territory` fields would have to be stamped at ingestion
from the tab identity, not read from a column. Supply chain has four
values (VMG, UMG, UMG & VMG, VMG & UMG). 214 ISRCs, 145 artists.

## Proposed raw schema

```
date            consumption date, not report date
artist          as reported, never normalised
track
isrc            join key
label
supply_chain
platform        stamped 'youtube' at ingestion — not in source
territory       stamped 'GB' at ingestion — not in source
consumption
source_report   report date — provenance, and the dedupe tiebreak
ingested_at
```

Primary key `date + isrc`. On collision, keep the row from the **latest**
`source_report` and log the difference — restatements are then visible
rather than silent. Never overwrite history; append and supersede.

## Storage and architecture

Raw daily rows in KV under `vmg:daily:{date}` (one hash per date, ISRC
as field), with `vmg:reports:{reportDate}` recording which dates each
report supplied. Derived windows — 7D/28D/13W/26W/YTD, ranks, entrants,
movers — computed on read from raw, never stored as the source of
truth.

At ~110 rows per date this is trivial in volume: a year is ~40,000 rows.

## Can Outlook be the source?

**Not directly, and it does not need to be.** The cleanest path uses
what already works: Leon sets an **Outlook rule to auto-forward
`Virgin Music Group Daily Trend Report` to his Gmail**, and a daily job
reads Gmail, which is already connected. No Microsoft integration, no
new credentials, no manual download.

A Microsoft Graph connector would be more direct but needs IT approval
for an app registration against a corporate tenant — slower, and the
rule gets us running tomorrow.

Backfill from 21 Sep onwards: the rolling-7-day property means the
**most recent report alone recovers a week**, and any seven consecutive
reports sitting in Outlook recover everything back to that point.

## Watcher changes required

Small: a Gmail reader, an attachment parser (pending format
confirmation), the KV store above, a daily cron after 12:05, and a
derived-windows module. No change to Chartmetric or to the existing
Watcher.
