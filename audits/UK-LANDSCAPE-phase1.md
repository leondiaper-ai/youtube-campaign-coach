# UK YouTube Landscape — Phase 1 investigation

Workbook audit, join feasibility, and a proposed data model for putting
`Virgin_UK_YouTube_MasterReview.xlsx` next to Watcher and Chartmetric.

**Nothing has been changed.** The workbook has not been written to, no
bulk Chartmetric calls have been made, and no UI has been built.

Date: 25 September 2026.

### Collaborations — the real matching problem

42 of 145 strings are collaborations and are deliberately kept whole.
The consequence is concentrated in one artist:

| String | 26W consumption |
|---|---|
| Dave, Central Cee | 1,260,147 |
| Dave | 1,257,602 |
| Dave, Burna Boy | 710,537 |
| Dave, Fredo | 561,453 |
| Dave, Stormzy | 257,134 |
| Dave, Ruelle | 96,570 |
| Dave, James Blake | 88,269 |
| James Blake, Dave | 85,719 |
| **Combined** | **4,317,431** |

Dave ranks **9th** as a solo string. Combined he would rank **2nd**,
above D-Block Europe. Note also that the last two rows are the same
pairing in two orders — order is not normalised in the source.

This is not a bug to fix silently. It is a judgement: whether featured
consumption counts toward an artist is a commercial question, and
Group A of NEEDS CONFIRMATION already asks it for 19 strings. But it
does mean **any ranking built on the raw strings understates Dave, and
could understate others the same way.**

The same class of problem, already documented in the workbook: Joan
Jett is split three ways by casing — "Joan Jett & the Blackhearts"
(459,720), "Joan Jett & The Blackhearts" (45,035) and "Joan Jett"
(9,467) — 514,222 combined, which would move her from rank 31 to
roughly 28.

### Other things found in the data

- **Three rows carry zero consumption**: K-Trap/Headie One (rank 143),
  John Summit/Absolutely (144) and Nickelback (145). They are in the
  report with a rank but no volume. Blank, not zero, is the right
  reading, and they should not be ranked in any new view.
- **`Digga` has a trailing space** in the Watcher channel title
  (`"Digga "`). Harmless here, a trap for a future exact match.
- **Two matches are Topic channels**, not owned channels: The Long
  Faces and The Royston Club. Their handles are raw `UC…` IDs. Topic
  channels are auto-generated by YouTube, so channel-scale metrics for
  them mean something quite different from an artist channel — The
  Royston Club "Topic" has 4,110 subscribers and 3.5m weekly views.
  They must stay flagged.
- **11 matched artists have no 7D view history**, shown blank. Blank is
  absence, never zero.

## 2 · How much can already be joined

Checked against the live Watcher roster (230 custom + 7 seed; last cron
sync 25 Sep 05:45, 233/233 successful) using the free KV-backed debug
route — **no Chartmetric requests were spent on this.**

| Source | Rows | In Watcher roster | With YouTube channel ID | With subscribers | With lifetime views |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CHANNEL PERFORMANCE | 24 | **24 / 24** | **24 / 24** | 24 / 24 | 24 / 24 |
| UK ARTISTS NOT IN DATA | 52 | **52 / 52** | **52 / 52** | 52 / 52 | 52 / 52 |
| **Total joinable** | **76** | **76** | **76** | **76** | **76** |

The two sets do not overlap: zero shared handles, as expected, since
the 52 are defined as having no consumption record.

**So all 76 can be deterministically resolved to Chartmetric** by the
route we proved on Ezra — channel ID → `/get-ids` → `cm_artist`. No
name search, no ambiguity.

The remaining 121 unmatched consumption strings have **no** channel and
therefore no deterministic path. They stay unmatched.

### A finding worth flagging on its own

**Watcher already holds lifetime channel views** (`rawViews`), which
the workbook does not currently use. It is present for all 76 and is a
better channel-scale measure than subscribers — XXXTENTACION's 43.5m
subscribers sit against 12.9bn lifetime views, while Underworld's
199,000 subscribers sit against 125m. Subscribers alone would rank
those two absurdly.

Also available per artist and unused by the workbook: 30-day uploads,
days since last upload, snapshot history depth, and 7-day subscriber
delta.

## 3 · Proposed definitions

These need to be visible wherever the numbers are, because four of them
sound alike and measure entirely different things.

| Field | Definition | Source | Scope | Period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **26W UK consumption** | UK YouTube consumption of the artist's repertoire captured in VMG internal supply-chain reporting. Not every UK YouTube view Virgin generates. | Daily Trend, YouTube tab | UK, repertoire-level | 23 Mar – 20 Sep 2026, fixed |
| **Chartmetric UK monthly views** | Rolling monthly YouTube views attributed to the artist in the United Kingdom by Chartmetric's YouTube-for-Artists data. | Chartmetric `market-coverage-views/youtube` | UK, artist-level | Rolling ~month, snapshot-dated |
| **Global channel subscribers** | Subscriber count of the owned channel. Global. Not consumption. | Watcher / YouTube Data API | Global, channel | Point-in-time |
| **Lifetime channel views** | Total views of the owned channel since creation. Global. | Watcher / YouTube Data API | Global, channel | Cumulative |
| **7D channel views** | Owned-channel views over the last 7 days, from Watcher snapshot history. Blank where history is absent. | Watcher snapshots | Global, channel | Rolling 7 days |
| **UK relative momentum** | UK's change between the two Chartmetric readings, expressed against the median change across that artist's returned territories. | Chartmetric | UK vs artist's own baseline | Observed window (~10 days on the Ezra reading) |

The load-bearing distinction: **26W consumption is repertoire in the UK
through our pipe. Chartmetric UK is the artist on YouTube in the UK.
Subscribers and lifetime views are the artist's channel, globally.**
A single blended score would destroy all three.

## 4 · The three Chartmetric UK measures

Following the methodology validated on Ezra Collective.

### UK Scale
`countries` row where `code2 === 'GB'` → `monthlyViews`. Straight read.
If there is no GB row, that is **"UK not in returned territories"** —
recorded as such, never as zero. Chartmetric returned 26 countries for
Ezra; a smaller artist may return fewer, and a UK absence from a short
list is a real signal.

### UK Importance
Two parts, both from the same response:
- **UK territory rank** — position of GB in the artist's countries
  sorted by monthly views. "UK is this artist's #2 market."
- **UK share of returned territories** — GB views ÷ sum of returned
  country views. Ezra's 26 countries summed to 1.42m against
  Chartmetric's own stated 1.9m monthly figure, so **this denominator
  is roughly three-quarters of the artist's total, not all of it.**
  The label has to say "of returned territories" every time, or it will
  be read as share of world views and quietly overstate.

### UK Relative Momentum
`(GB.value − GB.prev_value)` against the median `changePct` across the
artist's comparable territories — exactly the Ezra method.

The reason this is the interesting one: on Ezra, **almost every
territory roughly doubled** in the window, median +102%. The UK did
+101%. So the honest read is *"the UK moved with the artist's overall
lift, it is not a UK story"* — whereas Japan at +270% was. A raw UK
percentage with no baseline would have made the UK look like a
breakout. It wasn't.

Three caveats that must travel with these numbers:

1. The comparison window is **derived, not assumed** — `prev_value` is
   matched back against the city trends to find its date, and only
   accepted on a consensus of three or more rows. On Ezra that gave
   11 Sep → 21 Sep, ten days. A different artist may give a different
   window, and it must be shown per artist, not stated once globally.
2. `timestp` is **the day you asked**, not the reading date. Ezra's
   rows said 25 Sep for data measured on 21 Sep.
3. `is_estimate` — 11 of Ezra's 26 countries were modelled rather than
   measured. If GB ever comes back estimated, that changes how hard the
   number can be leaned on.

## 5 · Proposed combined data model

One row per **entity**, where an entity is either a consumption string
or a Watcher artist. Layers kept in separate column blocks, never
merged, never averaged.

```
IDENTITY
  entity_key, display_name, source (consumption | watcher | both)
  match_status  see §6
  watcher_slug, channel_handle, channel_id, is_topic_channel

LAYER 1 — UK CONSUMPTION            (blank for the 52 not in data)
  consumption_26w, consumption_rank, consumption_share
  last_4w, change_4w, change_13w
  tracks, top_track, top_track_share, supply_chain, is_collab_string

LAYER 2 — CHARTMETRIC UK            (blank where unresolved)
  cm_artist_id, cm_artist_name
  uk_monthly_views, uk_previous_views, uk_change_views, uk_change_pct
  uk_territory_rank, uk_share_of_returned, uk_is_estimate
  artist_median_change_pct, uk_vs_median_pp
  reading_date, previous_date, window_days, territories_returned

LAYER 3 — CHANNEL SCALE             (blank where not in Watcher)
  subscribers, lifetime_views, views_7d, uploads_30d
  days_since_upload, channel_state, campaign_phase, watcher_ownership

LAYER 4 — HUMAN JUDGEMENT           (empty by design)
  virgin_relationship, uk_signed_or_service, james_confirmed, notes
```

Layer 4 ships empty. Nothing writes to it but a person.

| `AMBIGUOUS` / `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` | Candidate found, not confident | 0 |

with a human check, not something to bulk-resolve — the workbook
records that loose matching previously sent **Morad** to a Persian
channel and **Twin S** to the Minnesota Twins, and both reached a
draft.

## 7 · Recommended first sample

**30 artists, chosen to test the comparison rather than to be
comprehensive.**

**A · Top 20 matched by 26W consumption** — the full set of matched
artists in rank order: Underworld, D-Block Europe, XXXTENTACION,
Childish Gambino, Marnz Malone, The Long Faces, French The Kid, Dave,
Jamie Webster, David Kushner, Cigarettes After Sex, beabadoobee,
Fontaines D.C., Van Morrison, Morgan Wallen, Whiskey Myers, Fredo,
Original Koffee, aespa, Digga.

This set is deliberately awkward, which is the point. It contains a
Topic channel (The Long Faces), a catalogue act with 199k subscribers
and the highest consumption in the dataset (Underworld), a global
superstar with 43.5m subscribers and no recent uploads (XXXTENTACION),
and a K-pop act that is plainly not UK repertoire (aespa). If the
three-measure model survives this set it will survive the roster.

**B · 10 high-scale Virgin UK artists with no consumption record** —
LAZARO, Peter Gabriel, CHVRCHES, Architects, Declan McKenna, IDLES,
Melanie C, Bloc Party, Ezra Collective, Luvcat.

Ezra is included on purpose as the control: we already know his answer,
so if the batch produces anything different for him, the batch is
wrong.

These are the artists most likely to expose the blind spot — real UK
signings with real channels and zero presence in the consumption
dataset.

At the client's 600 ms serial floor: **~36 seconds** for the sample,

correct.

Nothing should be run concurrently. The existing client serialises
through a promise chain, which already enforces this.

## 9 · Changes needed to the Chartmetric integration

Small, and all additive.

1. **A UK extractor** in `territories.ts` — `ukRow(result)` returning
   the GB row plus rank, share-of-returned and `uk_vs_median_pp`.
   Everything it needs is already in the normalized result.
2. **A batch route**, `/api/chartmetric/uk-batch`, taking a list of
   slugs and returning one UK row each. Sequential by construction
   because `cmFetch` already serialises. Needed because the current
   territories route is per-artist and tied to a page render.
3. **A durable UK snapshot** in KV, `cm:uk:{channelId}`, so the
   workbook build reads stored values rather than re-calling.

No change to `auth.ts`, `client.ts` or `resolve.ts`. There is one
Chartmetric implementation and it stays that way.

## 10 · What this combined dataset answers that the workbook cannot

The workbook answers one question well: *which repertoire generated the
most UK YouTube consumption in our reporting, over 26 weeks.* It cannot
answer any of the following, and each is a question someone actually
asks.

**Who has the biggest YouTube audience in the UK right now?**
The 26W figure is a six-month total through one pipe. Chartmetric UK
monthly views is current, artist-level, and independent of whether the
repertoire passed through our reporting at all.

**Which of our UK signings are invisible in our own reporting?**
52 Virgin UK artists have no consumption record whatsoever. Today the
only thing we can say about them is subscriber count. With UK monthly
views we can say how big they actually are *in the UK* — and a UK
signing with substantial UK YouTube views and no consumption record is
either a reporting gap or a repertoire gap, and both are worth knowing
which.

**Is this artist's UK position growing or is that just their overall
lift?** Nothing in the workbook can distinguish these. The relative
momentum measure can, and on Ezra it already changed the answer: the UK
was at the median, not ahead of it.

**How important is the UK to this artist?** A completely new axis. "UK
is their #1 market" and "UK is their #14 market" are different
commercial conversations, and neither is visible from consumption
volume or subscriber count.

**Where is consumption happening away from the owned channel?** High
26W consumption against low owned-channel scale is a specific,
checkable pattern — The Long Faces is 1.45m consumption on a Topic
channel with 46k subscribers, which says the audience is not on an
owned surface at all.

**Which artists over-index on the UK relative to their size?** Modest
global channel, high UK share of territories. Invisible today from
either dataset alone.

**And the one that is likely to be most useful:** the divergences. High
Chartmetric UK views with no consumption record at all is a blind spot
in the internal dataset. High consumption with a small UK Chartmetric
figure suggests the consumption is catalogue or licensed rather than
artist-led. Neither can be seen without both datasets side by side.

## What I am NOT proposing

- No combined score. The workbook's METHODOLOGY sheet is explicit that
  averaging the layers hides the distinctions, and adding Chartmetric
  makes that more true, not less.
- No automatic relationship inference. Layer 4 stays empty.
- No fuzzy resolution of the 121 unmatched strings.
- No change to the 26W consumption ranking. It remains authoritative
  for what it measures.
- No merging of the Dave strings or the Joan Jett strings. Flagged,
  quantified, left alone.
