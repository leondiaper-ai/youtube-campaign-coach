Five uploads: the Electric Lady performance, the Stone Pony film and its teaser, a Zane Lowe clip and the Annie DiRusso duet.
Two honest qualifications, then the point. First, this does not mean the 23.88M would have arrived with nothing happening — an album release, a tour announcement and a press cycle all pull people toward older videos, and that halo is real but invisible from outside. Second, the album's own singles — you and forever, the van, Dirty Wedding Dress — went up between February and mid-April, so they sit on the older side of the line.

What survives both is the shape: an album landed in the middle of this window and the overwhelming majority of viewing still went to things already published. A channel at 155,000 views a day rewards being organised, resurfaced and pointed at the right moments. The four opportunities that follow are all versions of that, and none of them requires shooting anything new.

What this does not show. The three songs with the full package include the two oldest and two biggest on the channel. Assets follow hits at least as readily as hits follow assets, and nothing here says the build made those songs big. The same caution applies to the four lyric videos: they have accumulated a great deal of viewing, but they are old and attached to the largest songs.

What is plainly true is that the habit stopped. Between 2014 and 2018 this channel built song ecosystems; since then a release gets a video and, at best, one live version. Worth choosing deliberately — and worth one controlled test.
Shorts · 94 uploads

Two Shorts worked.
Neither was a
concert clip.

Ninety-four Shorts have a median of 6,083 views. Two have cleared 50,000 and the next best is 46,996. What those two share is not the format — it is that you could not see either of them anywhere else.

152,565“the only live performance of ‘modern girl’ that exists” 144,905Tiny Moves — the choreography, by Margaret Qualley
A hypothesis worth testing, not a formula. One is billed as the only live performance of a song that exists. The other is a piece of choreography by the person in the music video. Neither is an excerpt of a show — and for context, the six Shorts cut from the 2023 Radio City performance sit between 1,841 and 6,083 views.

Two successes out of ninety-four is thin, and those six clips were published in a different year under different conditions, so this is not a controlled comparison. It is a reason to test distinctiveness as the variable — the thing nobody can see elsewhere — rather than to make more performance clips and expect a different result.
Where we would start

Four things.
Nothing new to shoot.

01

Give the Christmas song the assets it has never had — now, not in November

It is the channel's third-biggest music video, it charts every December, it grew between its first season and its second, and it is currently running at 5.4× its own daily average — all on one video. It has no lyric video and no official audio, and the two songs above it have both. That is the clearest unbuilt ecosystem in the catalogue, attached to the one song with a guaranteed annual moment.

Before November: add the missing assets, move the Annie DiRusso live version into season, link everything to everything, and chase the December 2025 television performance that never reached the channel. The season has already started — the ramp is measurable now.

Evidence · December slide — +400K in seven days, two assets against four, one chart week became four
02

Test individual song edits from the Stone Pony concert

Ninety-one minutes sit public as a single upload at 227,059 views — the album release show, played free the day after the record came out, to 900 people. Red Rocks is the precedent on this channel: three songs from one night, published separately, 383K views between them. Start with a handful and watch whether they add viewing or move it around. Subject to footage and to rights clearance, which the show being public does not by itself establish.

Evidence · Live slides — Red Rocks, and 28 single-song live videos
03

Test a complete support package on one upcoming song

Video, lyric video, official audio and a live version on a single release, measured against the video-only releases either side of it. The three songs that ever got this are the channel's strongest — but they are also its oldest, so that proves nothing on its own. One controlled release is what would turn the question into an answer, in either direction.

Evidence · Support slide — three full packages, none since 2018
04

Build Shorts around distinctive moments, not clips

The two Shorts that cleared 50,000 were a performance billed as the only one of its kind and a piece of choreography — not excerpts. Test that variable deliberately: a handful built on something unavailable elsewhere, measured against the 6,083 median. A creative experiment with a clear read-out, not a format to scale.

Evidence · Shorts slide — 152,565 and 144,905 against a 6,083 median
What this read cannot see. Everything here comes from public data — views, likes, comments, durations, dates and the UK chart record. Without Studio access there is no watch time, no retention, no traffic source and no audience breakdown, so nothing above is a claim about how long anyone watched or where they came from. Our own channel readings begin on 20 April 2026, and per-video history covers seven uploads. All four are framed as tests for that reason.
