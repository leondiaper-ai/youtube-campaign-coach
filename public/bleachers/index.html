It has a ninety-one minute concert film sitting whole on the shelf. This is a read of what that material is worth, and where a small amount of deliberate effort would go furthest.

Five uploads: the Electric Lady performance, the Stone Pony film and its teaser, a Zane Lowe clip and the Annie DiRusso duet. 651K views between them.
One distinction that matters, and then the point. This does not mean the 23.88M would have arrived with no activity at all. A release, a tour announcement or a press moment pulls people toward the older videos too, and that halo is real but invisible from outside — we can see where views landed, not what sent them there.

What it does establish is where the viewing lands: overwhelmingly on work that already exists. A channel at 155,000 views a day is a catalogue that rewards being organised, resurfaced and pointed at the right moments. The four opportunities that follow are all versions of that, and none of them requires shooting anything new.

What this does not show. The three songs with the full package include the two oldest and two biggest on the channel. Assets follow hits at least as readily as hits follow assets, and nothing here says the build made those songs big. The same caution applies to the four lyric videos: they have accumulated a great deal of viewing, but they are old and attached to the largest songs, so their totals are not evidence that a new lyric video would perform.

What is plainly true is that the habit stopped. Between 2014 and 2018 this channel built song ecosystems; since then a release gets a video and, at best, one live version. That is worth choosing deliberately — and worth one controlled test rather than an assumption either way.
Shorts · 94 uploads

Two Shorts worked.
Neither was a
concert clip.

Ninety-four Shorts have a median of 6,083 views. Two have cleared 50,000, and the next best is 46,996. What the two have in common is not the format — it is that you could not see either of them anywhere else.

152,565“the only live performance of ‘modern girl’ that exists” 144,905Tiny Moves — the choreography, by Margaret Qualley
A hypothesis worth testing, not a formula. One is billed as the only live performance of a song that exists. The other is a piece of choreography by the person in the music video. Neither is an excerpt of a show — and for context, the six Shorts cut from the 2023 Radio City performance sit between 1,841 and 6,083 views.

Two successes out of ninety-four is thin, and those six clips were published in a different year under different distribution conditions, so this is not a controlled comparison. It is a reason to test distinctiveness as the variable — the thing nobody can see elsewhere — rather than to make more performance clips and expect a different result.
Where we would start

Four things.
Nothing new to shoot.

01

Test individual song edits from the Stone Pony concert

Ninety-one minutes sit public as a single upload at 227,059 views. Red Rocks is the precedent on this channel: three songs from one night, published separately, 383K views between them. Start with a handful rather than the whole set, and watch whether they add viewing or move it around — some of it may come out of the full-show upload rather than on top of it. Subject to footage and to rights clearance, which the show being public does not by itself establish.

Evidence · Live slide — Red Rocks, and 28 single-song live videos
02

Prepare a recurring November–December campaign

Demand returns every year and grew between season one and season two on the UK chart. The material already exists: the 7.06M video, the Annie DiRusso live version currently sitting in August, and a television performance that never reached the channel. Resurface the video, move the live version into season, link the assets to each other, and fix late November in the calendar rather than reacting to it.

Evidence · December slide — one chart week became four
03

Test a complete support package on one upcoming song

Video, lyric video, official audio and a live version on a single release, measured against the video-only releases either side of it. The three songs that ever got this are the channel's strongest — but they are also its oldest, so that proves nothing on its own. One controlled release is what would turn the question into an answer, in either direction.

Evidence · Support slide — three full packages, none since 2018
04

Build Shorts around distinctive moments, not clips

The two Shorts that cleared 50,000 were a performance billed as the only one of its kind and a piece of choreography — not excerpts. Test that variable deliberately: a handful of Shorts built on something unavailable elsewhere, measured against the 6,083 median. Treat it as a creative experiment with a clear read-out, not a format to scale.

Evidence · Shorts slide — 152,565 and 144,905 against a 6,083 median
What this read cannot see. Everything here comes from public data — views, likes, comments, durations, dates and the UK chart record. Without Studio access there is no watch time, no retention, no traffic source and no audience breakdown, so nothing above is a claim about how long anyone watched or where they came from. Our own daily readings begin on 20 April 2026, which is why the December case rests on chart data. All four are framed as tests for that reason.
