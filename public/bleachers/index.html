The catalogue · what we can open and check
The 230 uploads on this channel, summed. Every percentage on this page is a share of this number, and each one can be traced to individual videos.

Unaccounted · lifetime counter reads 189.0M
The channel's lifetime counter is 78.0M higher than the uploads sum to. From public data we cannot say where that sits — removed or private videos, or counting differences on YouTube's side. No share on this page is taken against it.
Why we are strict about this. If the missing 78.0M is not spread evenly across formats, it would shift the percentages that follow — and we have no way to test that from outside. So the honest position is to measure only what can be inspected, say so every time, and let you judge the rest. Everything below is a statement about the 230 uploads, not about the channel counter.

This is the single most useful fact in the deck. A channel earning 155,000 views a day untouched is not a promotional channel that goes quiet between releases — it is a catalogue business that happens to also run campaigns. It means the highest-leverage work available is not making more things. It is making the things that already exist easier to find, better connected, and present at the moments when people are already looking for them.

The next three slides are the three clearest cases of exactly that.

What the channel did about it
Nov 2024 · video + 5 Shorts Nov–Dec 2025 · 2 Shorts Dec 2025 · TV performance, not uploaded Aug 2026 · live version published

Year one got a full launch. In year two — the bigger chart year — the channel posted two Shorts and no video asset. The band played the song on US network television in December 2025; that performance is not on the channel. And the one new Christmas asset made since, a live version with Annie DiRusso from the Stone Pony show, went up on 14 August.

What we can and cannot show. The chart record proves the demand returns and grew. It does not prove the YouTube views spike in December — our own daily snapshots on this channel only begin on 20 April 2026, so we have no observation of either Christmas period and are not going to estimate one. The recommendation that follows is therefore a plan to test against a known seasonal demand curve, not a claim about YouTube performance we have measured.

Read this carefully, because it cuts both ways. The three songs with the full set include the two oldest and two biggest on the channel, and a hit attracts assets as much as assets build a hit. Age and scale are tangled up in this, and nothing here shows that the build caused the size.

What it does show cleanly is a change in habit. Between 2014 and 2018 this channel built song ecosystems; since then, releases have had a video and, at best, one live version. Lyric videos are the clearest gap: four exist, they average 1.2M views each, and none has been made in eight years. That is worth choosing on purpose rather than by default.

Red Rocks, July 2024 — three songs from one show, published individually, 383,336 views between them. The Stone Pony show has never been cut up.
And one thing the live material does that the videos sometimes can't. Twelve songs have both an official video and a live version. In ten, the video leads comfortably — live is a supporting asset, not a substitute, and this deck will not pretend otherwise. But in two it doesn't: How Dare You Want More (live 601,311 vs video 304,157) and Dirty Wedding Dress (live 388,213 vs video 202,366).

Those two are precisely the two lowest-performing official videos in the catalogue. On the evidence here, live footage is not a way to beat a good video — it is a second chance at a song whose video didn't land, and it worked both times it was available.
Where we would start

Four things.
Nothing new to shoot.

Do first — the footage already exists
01

Cut the Stone Pony set into single-song videos

Ninety-one minutes are public as one upload at 227,050 views. Published as individual songs, that becomes the format with a 95,800-view median on this channel — and the Red Rocks precedent shows three songs from one show returning 383,336 between them. Subject to footage and rights, which we'd expect to be settled given the full show is already up. Keep the full show published alongside them — Red Rocks suggests the two serve different viewers, but that is the one part of this worth watching rather than assuming.

Finding · Performance footage: full length won, 95,800 vs 2,739
Then — book it before November
02

Put a December plan around the Christmas song

Demand returns every year and grew between year one and year two on the UK chart. The assets to work with already exist: the 7.06M video, the Annie DiRusso live version currently published in August, and a network TV performance that never made it to the channel. Resurface the video, move the live version into season, link the two to each other, and treat late November as a fixed date in the calendar rather than a reaction.

Finding · December: peak 65 in year two against 77 in year one
Then — one controlled test
03

Give one upcoming song the full build

Video, lyric video, official audio and a live version on a single release, measured against the video-only releases either side of it. Lyric videos are the specific gap — four exist, they average 1.2M views each, and the last was 2018. The three songs that got the full set are the channel's strongest, but they are also its oldest, so this is the test that would tell you whether the build matters or the songs did.

Finding · Song support: three full builds, last one 2018
04

Point Shorts at exclusive moments, not concert clips

Ninety-four Shorts have a median of 6,083 views and only two have cleared 50,000 — a Fallon performance billed as the only live version of Modern Girl that exists, and Margaret Qualley's Tiny Moves choreography. Neither is a concert clip; both are something you could not see anywhere else. The six straight live clips from Radio City sit at a 2,739 median. Test the exclusivity angle, and stop treating Shorts as offcuts.

What this read cannot see. Everything here comes from public data — views, likes, comments, durations, dates, and the UK chart record. Without Studio access there is no watch time, no retention, no traffic source and no audience breakdown, so nothing above is a claim about how long anyone watched or where they came from. Our own daily readings on this channel begin on 20 April 2026, which is why the December case is built on chart data and not on view curves. The four recommendations are framed as things to test for exactly that reason.
