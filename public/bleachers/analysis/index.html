The uploads sum to 111,033,880. The channel counter reads 188,997,142. The 77,963,262 difference — 41.3% — cannot be accounted for from public data; it may sit in removed or private videos, or in counting differences at YouTube's end. Every percentage on the deck is a share of the 111.0M, and nothing is ever divided by the lifetime figure.

The limitation this creates. If the missing 78M is not spread evenly across formats, the format shares below would shift — and there is no way to test that from outside. The shares are exact statements about the catalogue we can open. They are estimates about the channel.

Public counters move through the day. Read at 15:20 UTC the catalogue summed to 111,033,880; earlier the same day, 111,031,833. Figures on the deck are rounded for that reason.

The five: Dirty Wedding Dress live at Electric Lady (388,227), the Stone Pony film (227,059), the Annie DiRusso Christmas duet (25,020), the Stone Pony teaser (5,647) and a Zane Lowe clip (4,681).

Two qualifications that matter more than the headline.

First, this does not mean the 23.88M would have arrived with nothing happening. everyone for ten minutes was released on 22 May 2026 — inside this window. A release, a tour announcement and a press cycle all pull people toward older videos, and that halo is real but invisible from outside. We can see where views landed, not what sent them there.

Second, the album's own singles — you and forever (11 Feb), the van (13 Apr), Dirty Wedding Dress (17 Apr) — went up before the window opened, so they sit on the "older material" side of the line.

What survives both: an album landed in the middle of this window and the overwhelming majority of viewing still went to things already published. That is a claim about where viewing lands, not about what would have happened otherwise.

7,060,665 views across 672 days is a lifetime average of 10,507 a day. 400,000 across seven days is 57,143 a day — 5.4× its own norm, in September.

What this ranks, and what it does not. These are the seven uploads we hold per-video history for, not all 230. So "the fastest-moving video on the channel" is not established — the older catalogue has no per-video series to compare. What is solid is the video measured against its own history, which is the comparison the deck makes.

What we have not measured

Our daily readings begin 20 April 2026. We have no observation of either previous Christmas period and the deck does not estimate one. The claim that demand returns every year rests entirely on the UK chart record, which is independent of YouTube — and the chart graphic on the deck is labelled as chart position for exactly that reason.

What the channel published each season

Season one: the official music video and five Shorts. Season two — the bigger chart year — two Shorts and no video asset. A December 2025 network television performance was not published to the channel; the catalogue shows no upload at all between 31 October 2025 and 3 February 2026. The one new Christmas asset since is the Annie DiRusso live version, published 14 August 2026.

Live performance

These are also the two lowest-viewed official music videos in the catalogue. The deck's claim is therefore narrow: live is a second opening for a song whose video didn't land. It is explicitly not a ranking of formats.

Stone Pony: 91 minutes, 227,059 views, published 5 June 2026, never split into songs. Red Rocks precedent, 8 July 2024: three songs from one night published individually — 171,061 + 143,285 + 68,986 = 383,332. The channel holds 28 single-song live videos in total.

Two things the Stone Pony recommendation does not assume. That individual uploads would purely add viewing — they may redistribute it from the full-show upload, and that is stated on the deck. And that the footage is cleared: a concert film being public establishes nothing about permission to cut it up.

Song support

Songs with an official video and a lyric video and an official audio and a live version: three — I Wanna Get Better (30.67M), Rollercoaster (18.92M), Alfie's Song (3.72M). The most recent was 2018.

Every total in the deck's support table counts those same four asset types and nothing else, which is why a song's figure there is lower than its all-upload total. The "you and forever" official video alone is 2,198,689 of its 2,750,183.

Four lyric videos exist: I Wanna Get Better (3,714,016), Alfie's Song (563,167), Shadow (480,248), Rollercoaster (128,413). Shadow has a lyric video, an audio and a live version but no official music video, which is why it is not in the support table.

The confound, stated plainly. The three songs with the full package include the two oldest and two biggest on the channel. Assets follow hits at least as readily as hits follow assets. Nothing here shows the build made those songs big — and the same caution applies to the lyric videos, whose totals are not evidence that a new one would perform. That is why the recommendation is a controlled test rather than an assertion.

Shorts

94 Shorts, median 6,083 views. Two above 50,000 — 152,565 (billed as the only live performance of Modern Girl that exists) and 144,905 (Margaret Qualley's choreography). The third best is 46,996. Neither top performer is a concert excerpt.

For context only: the six Shorts cut from the 2023 Radio City performance run 1,841 to 6,083. Those were published in a different year under different distribution conditions, so this is not a controlled comparison, and two successes out of ninety-four is thin. The deck frames it as a creative hypothesis — test distinctiveness as the variable — not a formula.

What this deck does not claim

  1. That the 23.88M would have arrived with no activity at all.
  2. Any measured YouTube seasonality — we have no observation of either past Christmas.
  3. That live outperforms official video. Across twelve songs, the video leads in ten.
  4. That building assets caused the older songs' size.
  5. That a public concert film may be cut up without clearance.
  6. Any watch time, retention, traffic source or demographic figure.
  7. Any percentage taken against the 189M lifetime counter.

Open questions we cannot answer from here

  1. Redistribution. If the Stone Pony set is split into songs, do those add viewing or draw it from the full-show upload? Only running it would tell you.
  2. Cross-platform inference. Recommendation 01 assumes December chart demand is reachable on YouTube. That is an inference across platforms — reasonable, but not proven by anything here.
  3. The halo. The 97.3% is unadjustable for the lift an album release gives older videos, because that lift is invisible from outside.
  4. Season-two output. We state two Shorts, which is what reached the channel. Promotion may have run elsewhere — television, playlists, DSPs — and we cannot see it.
  5. Classification. Formats are assigned from titles. A mislabelled upload is counted as what it says it is.
