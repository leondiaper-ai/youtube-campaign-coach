Every view figure on both pages is a live counter read at that moment and drifts by a few hundred within the hour; they are accurate to the stated read time, not frozen. 84.3% coverage is unusually good for this kind of read, so share-of-catalogue statements stand on firmer ground than they often do. The remaining 522,044,141 views sit in content that is no longer public, and nothing here claims to account for it.

The two limitations

1 · ESTRUS and Dirt Femme view totals are not comparable

A matched campaign clock puts assets published in the same relative window side by side. But Dirt Femme's assets were published in 2022 and have been accumulating for about four years; ESTRUS's have had between seventeen days and five months.

A matched window of publication is not a matched window of accumulation. And it cannot be corrected: the API returns a lifetime counter and nothing else, so there is no way to ask what Dirt Femme had done at day 17 — nobody recorded it in 2022.

So the deck compares structure (cadence, format mix, how each visual world was built) and shares, which age does not distort. No cross-era view total appears anywhere on it.

2 · There is no ESTRUS release-week velocity data

The Watcher's daily collection for this channel failed during the week that mattered:

  • The channel snapshot has been frozen since 22 September at 3,330,599,183 views and 3,880,000 subscribers — thirteen days with no movement recorded.
  • The per-video daily series stops on 17 September, the day before the album.
  • Earlier readings are catch-ups, not daily: 8 September records +33,582,602 in a single reading covering fourteen days.

Every ESTRUS figure in the deck is therefore a lifetime total as at 5 October, with no visible shape behind it. The deck makes no claim about momentum, velocity, first-week performance or growth rate, because any such claim would have been invented.

The deck frames this as audience selection, not as ten failures. Seventeen days is not long, every track was given the same surface on the same day, and one has moved. That is a signal about demand, not a verdict on ten pieces of work.

des fleurs — sequence, not cause

Visualizer 12 June, 899,673. Official video 30 June, 4,978,233. Eighteen days apart.

The deck says only that an early visual treatment did not exhaust demand for a larger hero asset. It does not say the visualizer caused the video's performance — nothing in public data can show whether it helped, hurt or did nothing.

Seven distinct long-form formats appear in the campaign: official video, visualizer, alternate-cut visualizer, lyric video, behind the scenes, remix audio and live performance, plus the 33-minute album film. Three songs received more than one of them.

This is the test of our own earlier finding. The original case study flagged five formats in just over two weeks around the lead single and read it as a multi-format rollout. That is confirmed — official video 13 May, BTS 26 May, Her Cut visualizer 29 May, lyric video 3 June, remix 5 June, all inside twenty-three days. What the full window adds is that it describes how the lead single was handled, not how the campaign was run. The deck says so rather than repeating the original slide.

Cadence and follow-through

58 uploads in the window: 38 Shorts, 20 long-form, on 28 distinct days. Shorts appear on 27 of those days, long-form on 10; 18 days carry a Short and nothing else. That is what supports "Shorts created frequency" as a description of publishing behaviour. It is not a claim that Shorts sent anyone to the long-form.

Premiere use — verifiable, and consistent

Premiere status is normally the thing we have to leave out, but it is recoverable here. The API returns scheduledStart and actualStart, and a Premiere shows a scheduled time with the broadcast starting within seconds of it. Three uploads in the whole campaign carry that signature:

What this read does not claim

  • That ESTRUS is out- or under-performing Dirt Femme. See limitation 1.
  • Anything about release-week momentum. See limitation 2.
  • That the quiet year cost anything. One upload in 2025 is a fact; its consequences are not visible from outside.
  • That the des fleurs visualizer drove the official video. Sequence only.
  • That the Scenes approach beat the visualizer approach, or the reverse. They are measured at four years and seventeen days.
  • Anything about streaming, radio or touring. No DSP data was used; YouTube views are not a proxy for consumption elsewhere.
  • That Shorts drove viewing to the long-form. Shorts created publishing frequency; whether anyone moved between the two is not visible in public data.
  • That the two silences cost anything. 43 days before the album and 17 days after it are facts about what was published. What they did or did not cost is not measurable from outside, and the post-album row is graded open for exactly that reason.
  • That more surfaces per song produce more viewing. The format depth table counts what each song was given, not what it earned.
  • That footage of any specific song exists from A Night In ESTRUS. The crew credits on the DNH upload are verifiable. What else was performed, whether it was filmed, whether it is usable and whether rights allow release are all unknown — no setlist for the event is public. The deck recommends an audit of the rushes, and claims nothing about what they contain.
  • That the decision bands are objective categories. The top band is unambiguous — an 88% gap to second place. The line under the next three falls on a 32% drop, which is a real break but not the only one in the ranking; drawn slightly differently the middle band would hold more or fewer songs. They are a way to decide how much to spend, not a measurement.
  • That source of life has earned anything. It is the clearest candidate for a second moment. Seventeen days with no velocity data is not enough to know its curve.

Why this page is longer than the deck. The deck carries the argument; this page carries the caveats. Qualifying footnotes were removed from the slides in the final editorial pass — a presentation that annotates itself is harder to follow, and a reader who wants the limits of a claim is better served by having them collected here than by reading them in six-point type under a chart. Nothing was dropped: every qualifier that limited a claim either moved into the slide copy itself or is stated above.
