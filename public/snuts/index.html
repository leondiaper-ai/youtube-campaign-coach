How to read the confidence labels

HIGH means several independent sources point the same way. MEDIUM means the evidence is real but thin or confounded. LOW means we're acting on a hunch and should say so. No percentages — we don't have enough campaigns yet to earn them.

The Spotify lean-in rule · rebuilt from Snuts evidence only

Summer Rain is the only release with audience-quality data and the only one that produced a follower step-change. A focus track earns a controlled paid test when several audience-quality signals together approach or exceed the strongest behaviour this campaign has produced.

SIGNAL multiple signals near the Summer Rain benchmark AUDIENCE light & moderate listeners OBJECTIVE followers and pre-saves MEASURE follower gain per 1,000 reached vs 41/day organic STOP doesn't beat organic → stop, don't scale

No numeric entry trigger · save rate and SPL exist for one of five releases, in two conflicting readings. The 41/day median is Snuts-derived and verified, so the measurement is sound even though the entry threshold is not. Budget · CONTROLLED TEST — BUDGET REQUIRES PAID PERFORMANCE DATA. Platform playbook · 02

On YouTube, two asset types have earned priority. Three haven't.

Earned it
  • Conversation / collaboration — podcast8,995/day · 5.66%
  • Official music video — PTS, Summer Rain7,000 & 740/day

Both official videos are in the campaign's top four assets by rate. The podcast is the only asset that provably reached people with no prior relationship to the band.

Hasn't earned it
  • Lyric video65 & 62/day
  • Stripped / acoustic / live cut29–33/day
  • Shorts at volumemedian 1,087 lifetime

High like rates (2.2–4.4%) on tiny reach. The core loves these. They do not travel, and they have not moved a release.

The day-3 rule · the most reusable thing this campaign taught us

Do not judge a release before day 5. Judge the direction of daily gain across days 4–8, not the volume at day 3 — and check whether the rest of the channel moved.

PTS day 3 1,698/day → day 8 10,250/day Catalogue across the same window +13 views/day, combined

If the rest of the channel lifts too, the movement is channel-wide. If only the one video lifts, the movement appears video-specific — which is a reason not to spend into it, but not evidence of which surface delivered it. That needs YouTube Studio traffic sources.

Paid trigger for YouTube

Leave it alone while daily gain is rising. A video earns amplification only once gain has flattened and engagement has held. Stop if daily gain falls below ~3,000 for three consecutive days — that's the reassess point, not the spend point.

The focus-track playbook · built from this campaign only

What we should have ready, and when.

Before release
Build the stack, not the schedule
  • Official video ready — not a lyric video
  • A live or PR moment inside the same 72 hours
  • Podcast ep.2 scheduled into the week, not after it
  • Pre-save route on every asset before anything publishes
  • Local casting booked on the hero asset
Release day
One moment, everything at once
  • Hero: official video, premiered
  • Spotify: Countdown Page is the primary ask
  • YouTube: route in the first line of every description
  • TikTok: the sound, not the pre-save
  • Instagram: the news, with the album link
  • Commercial: placements confirmed, not chased
Days 2–4
Measure. Do not spend.
  • Save rate and SPL against the Summer Rain benchmark
  • Follower gain against the 41/day median
  • YouTube daily gain — direction, not volume
  • Whether the rest of the channel moved
  • TikTok creation rate against PTS's 1.40
Day 5
The earliest honest decision point
  • PTS's trough was day 3 at 1,698/day
  • By day 8 it was 10,250/day
  • A day-3 decision would have been wrong
  • Branch from here — next slide
The single biggest change from how we ran the last five

Four of the five singles were released on their own, with satellite assets trailing behind them, and four of the five moved nothing. Both times this campaign moved more than one platform, several things landed inside the same few days.

Days 5–7 · four branches, decided by what the signals say

Four outcomes. Four different responses.

A · Converting
Audience-quality signals near the Summer Rain benchmark · follower gain clearly above median · YouTube gain rising
Spend — on capture, not reach. Spotify paid to light and moderate listeners, pointed at the pre-save. Leave YouTube organic alone while it's still climbing. This is the branch we have never actually been in since January.
B · Attention, no capture
YouTube and TikTok strong · followers at or near median · saves soft
Don't buy more reach. This is the PTS pattern and the August pattern. Fix routes, push the Countdown Page, get episode two out. Spending here pours water into the same bucket.
C · Response above support
Listener movement well ahead of the support the track received
This is the Motherlands shape. It converted followers at only 1.34× a median day, so it is not a capture success — but it moved listeners on almost no distribution. Take it to Commercial as a support case before spending.
D · Neither
Flat on every measure by day 7 · the In Motion shape
Stop, don't rescue. In Motion got a lyric video, a stripped cut and a live video and its three assets do 124 views/day between them. Protect the album moment instead of spending into a flat release.
The rule that governs all four

No branch is chosen before day 5, and no branch is chosen on volume alone. Direction of travel and whether the rest of the channel moved are what separate an organic lift from a moment that's already over.
