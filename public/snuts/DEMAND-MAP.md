# JOY IN SHORT MOMENTS — CAMPAIGN DEMAND MAP
### Summer Rain → PTS · where demand is, what's driving it, what to do before 23 October
**v2 · 5 October 2026 · 18 days to album day**

**Scope.** The campaign is the unit of analysis. It starts at Summer Rain (30 January 2026).
Catalogue appears only as context, benchmark or downstream behaviour — never as a recommendation.

**What changed from v1.** Age-matching the five releases **reversed** one of my conclusions and
killed another. Both corrections are in the working. The headline reversal: on a like-for-like
basis PTS is the campaign's **strongest** TikTok performer, not its weakest. I had compared a
10-day-old track to a six-year-old one, which is exactly the error I've been flagging elsewhere.

## AUDIENCE — core or expanding?

**Expanding on Spotify. Core everywhere else.**

Monthly listeners +56.2% since 1 January (499,500 → 780,062) against Spotify followers +7.5%,
Instagram +4.0%, TikTok followers +2.6%. The followers-to-listeners ratio fell 35.9% → 24.1%.

Listening is growing far faster than any durable-capture metric we can observe. The podcast is the
single clearest proof the campaign *can* reach non-fans — and the clearest proof it isn't holding
them: channel subscribers moved **+100** the week it landed.

## INVESTMENT

### HOLD — PTS
Organic velocity still rising at day 10 (7,725/day). Best DSP position of the campaign. Spending
now buys views it is getting free and destroys the read.
**Trigger to revisit:** daily gain below ~3,000 for three consecutive days.

### SCALE — the podcast format into album week
Campaign's best asset per day (8,995) and best engagement rate (5.66%, 13× the single's). It is
the only proven route to non-fans. Put the album ask in the first 30 seconds, not below 400
characters of prose — which is where it currently isn't.
**Measure:** like rate holds above 2% **and** the share of comments mentioning the band or album
rises against episode one.

### TEST — Spotify-side capture, album week
The August audience never appeared on a surface The Snuts control. Spotify's own surfaces are the
only route to them.
**Measure:** follower gain per 1,000 reached against the 41/day organic baseline. **Not streams.**
**Stop:** if it doesn't beat organic, stop — do not scale on a stream number.

### INVESTIGATE — Motherlands with Spotify
Zero editorials, smallest footprint of the campaign, second-best listener week of the five. Costs
a conversation.
**Caveat to take into the room:** YouTube disagrees — 12,525 views in 193 days. Spotify and
YouTube tell different stories about this song.

### STOP
1. **Shorts at volume.** 44 → 49,232 views → 37 comments. Fix the links because it's free; don't expect it to move the album.
2. **Lyric videos as the default single asset.** 65 and 62 views/day. Both official-grade videos are in the campaign's top four assets.
3. **Broad reach buying.** Reach is the working part.

## DISCONNECTED SIGNALS

1. **Management believes discovery is weak. Spotify says it's expanding fast.** Every surface read daily — subscribers, socials, comments — is one where only committed fans act. *Decision: stop buying reach to fix discovery; the gap is capture.*
2. **Marketing read PTS as underperforming at day 3. It was about to accelerate 6×.** Catalogue unmoved throughout, so it wasn't PR. *Decision: agree an evaluation window before album day.*
3. **Commercial is investigating the NMF UK miss on the best-supported single. Motherlands got zero Spotify editorial and nobody noticed.** *Decision: bring Motherlands to the same conversation.*
4. **The album's conversion strategy lives on the format only existing fans see.** The June decision to split Spotify and Apple CTAs was right and is implemented on long-form. 0 of 44 Shorts carry it; 22 have no description at all, including the album and presale announcements. *Decision: fix it, but size the prize honestly — 49,232 mostly-core views.*
5. **Social is measured on followers. The campaign's biggest audience never saw social.** *Decision: you cannot capture an audience you never met — the lever is on Spotify.*

## UNKNOWN — what blocks a decision

1. **How did the August audience arrive?** Spotify for Artists → Audience → source split. Without it, "capture them" has no target. Highest-value missing number in the campaign.
2. **Where did PTS's day-4-to-8 views come from?** YouTube Studio → traffic sources, daily. Settles organic vs PR and tells us what to expect on album day.
3. **Is the pre-save funnel working at all?** Exposure → click → Countdown Page → pre-save, by source. The campaign's stated conversion strategy is currently unmeasured.

# THE WORKING

## 1 · What changed when I age-matched

**Correction 1 — TikTok, reversed.** v1 said PTS had 14 TikTok videos against Glasgow's 809 and
called it a weakness. Glasgow is five years old. Per day of life:

| | PTS | In Motion | Defibrillator | Summer Rain | Motherlands |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Videos/day | **1.40** | 0.68 | 0.41 | 0.37 | 0.22 |

PTS is the campaign's **strongest** TikTok performer, 3.8× the lead single's rate.
**Caveat:** creation rates are highest in a track's first days, so PTS is flattered and no TikTok
time series exists to correct for it. Treat the ranking as directional. Motherlands at 0.22 is the
genuine laggard — oldest-but-one and lowest rate.

**Correction 2 — the catalogue regression is withdrawn from the map.** The popularity-vs-reach
residual analysis put *Always* (+13.3) at the top of a recommendation list for an album campaign.
It answers a different question and it let a 2020 song dominate. Removed from every ranking.

What survives from it, campaign-only: **Motherlands had the second-highest demand-above-exposure
score of any track in the catalogue** — popularity 41 on 30,305 reach against a predicted 29.1.
That is the one campaign-relevant output and it is corroborated by the age-matched follower and
listener figures above.

## 2 · Why each release performed as it did

### SUMMER RAIN · 30 Jan · the benchmark nothing has matched
**Support:** 1 Spotify editorial, 170,016 playlist reach, 1 Amazon editorial. An **official video**.
**Response:** followers +1,453 in 9 days (3.94× median), with +582 and +581 on 31 Jan–1 Feb alone.
Monthly listeners +30,843 in 7 days, 18th-biggest week of 270 in 2026. Internal February read: save
rate 34.6%, streams-per-listener 1.74.
**What we learn:** this is the only release that converted at scale, and it had the fullest stack —
a proper video, a release *weekend*, and a band returning after a quiet 2025. Everything since has
been measured against a bar set by a campaign-opening moment.

### MOTHERLANDS · 26 Mar · demand without distribution
**Support:** **zero** Spotify editorials. 30,305 playlist reach — 5.6× less than Summer Rain,
39.8× less than the biggest catalogue footprint. 1 Apple editorial. A lyric video.
**Response:** followers +495 over 9 days (1.34× median) — but release *day* itself was +41, exactly
the median. Listeners +17,831 in 7 days, **43rd of 270 — second-best of the five**.
**What we learn:** the strongest demand-versus-support gap in the campaign. The song found people
without a push. Its YouTube asset is the campaign's weakest (12,525 in 193 days), so Spotify and
YouTube disagree — which is itself worth understanding before album week.

### DEFIBRILLATOR · 16 Jun · support did its job and didn't transform
**Support:** the campaign's best editorial week. NMF UK #44, NMF Japan #52, The Indie List #41,
Rock The World #7 JP; Apple New in Alternative #6 across 61 territories, New in Rock #3; Amazon
Fresh Indie #1 + cover; YouTube RELEASED #23.
**Response:** followers +609 over 9 days (1.65× median) — the best of the four post-lead singles.
Release day itself +36, below median. Listeners +11,142 in 7 days, 97th of 270. Its lyric video has
81,194 views, 6.5× the other two lyric videos.
**What we learn:** heavy editorial support lifts the asset (81K on a lyric video is real) and
produces a modest, not transformative, conversion effect. It cannot be the whole plan for album week.

### IN MOTION · 23 Jul · the one with nothing around it
**Support:** 1 Spotify editorial, 174,221 reach, 1 Amazon. Lyric video, plus a stripped-back cut
and a premiered Berlin live video.
**Response:** weakest of all five on every measure. Followers +408 (1.11× median — statistically a
normal fortnight). Listeners +3,895 in 7 days, **167th of 270**. Lyric video 4,516 views in 74 days.
**What we learn:** it was released into the middle of the festival run with no stacked moment, and
it is the clearest evidence that a single plus its satellite assets, on its own, does nothing.
The three In Motion assets between them do 124 views/day.

### PTS · 25 Sep · the best-supported release, still open
**Support:** best of the campaign on every DSP — 1,207,306 Spotify playlist reach (6.9× the next
single), popularity 47 (vs a 41–43 band), 2 Apple editorials, 2 Amazon, 1 Deezer carrying 134,748.
Amazon Brand New Music ≈#9. **No NMF UK.** An **official video**.
**Response:** daily YouTube gain 1,698 at day 3 → 10,250 at day 8, with no second push. 42.4% of
the channel's entire trailing week. Followers +511 over 9 days (1.38× median). Listeners +11,899 in
7 days, 89th of 270 — while fighting a headwind no other single faced, because the 28-day window
was still shedding the August peak. TikTok likes 8.1× baseline. Highest TikTok creation rate of the
campaign.
**What we learn:** the acceleration is **video-specific** — across the same window the four other
campaign assets gained **13 views a day between them**. A PR or brand event lifts the catalogue;
this didn't. It is too young to judge on conversion, and it should not be spent against yet.

## 3 · Catalogue as downstream behaviour — did PTS create artist discovery?

The right use of catalogue here is as a test of depth: when a new release lands, do people go
exploring?

| Window | Catalogue + pre-campaign views | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| 21–28 Sep (PTS days 1–3) | 50,539 | 7,220 |
| 28 Sep – 5 Oct (PTS days 3–10) | 52,995 | 7,571 |

**+4.9%. Essentially flat.** While PTS itself went from 11,338 to 65,917 views, the rest of the
channel did not move.

**Reading:** PTS is generating *song* consumption, not yet *artist* discovery. Over 30 days the
catalogue is still 64.3% of all channel views (178,553 of 277,716) — a stable engine running
underneath the campaign rather than responding to it.

**Caveat:** we cannot see *which* catalogue videos those views land on or how anyone reaches them,
so "no lift in aggregate" does not rule out movement within the catalogue. YouTube Studio closes this.

## 4 · Campaign creative — the 10× cliff

Two assets in this campaign run at 7,000–9,000 views/day. Everything else is under 750, and most
is under 70. The two are an official music video and a long-form conversation with a collaborator.

The inverse relationship matters as much as the ranking. Assets above 700/day have like rates of
0.31–0.45%; assets below have 2.2–8.1%. The campaign's most *loved* content — direct-to-camera
band banter (3.97%), the acoustic and stripped cuts (2.9–4.4%), the BTS film (8.06%) — is seen by
almost nobody. The content that reaches people is engaged with weakly, which is the signature of
an audience that didn't come looking.

**That is a programming instruction, not a creative one:** the campaign makes a lot of content the
core loves and few people see, and very little of the kind that travels.

## 5 · Standing caveats

1. **No age-matched YouTube comparison between the singles is possible.** The per-video daily series begins 15 September 2026, so only PTS has early-life data. Every other single's YouTube figure is a lifetime counter. The views/day ranking in §4 is a *rate*, not a trajectory, and it flatters young assets.
2. **Spotify's daily listener series cannot be read day-by-day** — Sunday averages 3.95× Thursday all year. Weekly resolution only. Followers pass the same test and are used daily.
3. **Playlist reach and `sp_popularity` are current readings** and both favour recent releases. PTS's 1,207,306 is at peak-of-life; Summer Rain's 170,016 has decayed over 248 days. Do not read the DSP table as a like-for-like of support *at release*.
4. **TikTok follower and like series are 69% and 72% interpolated.** Coarse comparisons only. Instagram is 0% interpolated and reliable.
5. **Territory movement is UNKNOWN.** Chartmetric returns a current city snapshot with no history.
6. **No paid-spend data of any kind was available**, so nothing here separates organic from bought.
7. **Causality is not claimed.** The festival explanation for August was tested against real dates (TRNSMT 20 Jun, Kendal Calling 31 Jul, Y Not 30 Jul–2 Aug) and withdrawn.
