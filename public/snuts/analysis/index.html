One reconciliation that does not close

The 310 returned uploads sum to 11,722,939 lifetime views against a channel counter of 18,177,485 — a gap of 6,454,546, or 35.5%. That gap is not explained by anything we can see from outside: it may be deleted or privatised videos, or Shorts-feed views counted differently at channel level.

Consequence: no figure in the deck is expressed as a share of the channel's lifetime views. All share figures are shares of a measured delta over a stated window, where the numerator and denominator come from the same window.

02Rules this analysis follows

  1. No age-mismatched comparison. A 10-day-old video is never ranked against a 248-day-old one. Where only lifetime counters exist, the figures are shown side by side with their ages visible and explicitly labelled as not a performance ranking.
  2. Attribution windows must match. Every share of channel consumption uses the identical start and end dates for the numerator and the denominator — 28 September baseline, 5 October reading.
  3. Interpolated values are never a claim on their own. Chartmetric interpolates some daily points. Every figure quoted here either falls on an observed reading or is a difference across a span long enough that interpolation within it cannot change the sign or magnitude. TikTok likes before mid-June increment by exactly 917 every week, which is interpolation, and are not used.
  4. Correlation is labelled. Everything is tagged Fact, Interpretation, Hypothesis or Gap. A timing coincidence is never written as a cause.
  5. Internal context is treated as supplied, not verified. The February Spotify figures, the June placement report, the PTS commercial tracking and the New Music Friday miss come from the brief. They are used as context and are never recomputed or contradicted — but where live data bears on their interpretation, that is stated.

05The central hypothesis, tested

"The Snuts have a strong established audience, but the Joy In Short Moments campaign may currently be better at activating existing fans than expanding beyond them."

Evidence that supports it

  • Fact Every owned follower base is near-flat across nine months: Spotify followers +7.5%, Instagram +4.0%, TikTok followers +2.6%.
  • Fact YouTube subscribers: +200 in 30 days against 277,716 views. 0.72 per 1,000 — the Watcher's WEAK band, unchanged across both the 7-day and 30-day windows.
  • Fact Comment language is returning-fan language. Eight variants of "we're so back" on the Summer Rain thread; essentially no discovery language in 158 music comments.
  • Fact YouTube is the band's smallest owned platform by a wide margin — Spotify followers are 6.4× the subscriber count, Instagram 4.5×, TikTok 3.6×.

Evidence that contradicts it

  • Fact Spotify monthly listeners 499,500 → 780,062, +280,562 (+56.2%). An already-activated core cannot produce that; it is already listening.
  • Fact The followers-to-listeners ratio fell from 35.92% to 24.08%. The listening pool outgrew the committed pool by a third.
  • Fact 587,761 current monthly listeners are not followers — 75.3% of the listening audience.
  • Fact PTS sits on Spotify playlists reaching 1,207,306, 6.9× the next single in this campaign, with the campaign's highest popularity score (47 vs a 41–43 band).
  • Fact The podcast episode drew a demonstrably non-Snuts audience: of 60 comments read, the overwhelming majority concern Louis Tomlinson, several in Spanish and Portuguese, referencing his own tour dates.

Verdict

The hypothesis is half right, and the wrong half is the half that matters. Expansion is happening and it is large. Capture is not happening at all.

The internal reading formed for a sound reason: every surface the team looks at daily — YouTube comments, subscriber counts, social follower numbers — is a surface on which only committed fans act. Reading those surfaces and inferring reach from them produces exactly the conclusion management reached. The reach metrics say something different.

Strategic consequence. The intervention this implies is not "buy more reach". It is "capture the reach we have" — and those are opposite spends.

Why a high algorithmic share is not proof of discovery

The brief is right to flag this, and the live data lets us go one step further than the warning. For an established artist, Release Radar and artist radio surface new music to people who already follow — so algorithmic share conflates core delivery with discovery and cannot separate them.

The followers-to-listeners ratio does separate them. If algorithmic consumption were predominantly core, listeners and followers would move together and the ratio would hold. It fell by a third. That is the cleanest discovery evidence available without Spotify's own segment data. Hypothesis

10Measurement gaps

These are the things we could not see. None has been estimated anywhere in the deck or this document.

Gap 01 — Where the catalogue views come from Highest value

What's missing: 178,553 views in 30 days landed on material published before 5 September. We cannot see which videos, or how anybody got to them.

Why it matters: if a measurable share of those views comes from Browse or Suggested rather than from subscribers, the channel is doing discovery work nobody is counting — and the right album-week programming looks completely different.

Who holds it: YouTube Studio → Content, last 30 days, plus the Traffic Sources report. One export.

Gap 02 — The pre-save funnel

What's missing: exposure → click → Countdown Page → pre-save, split by source. The brief asks for it explicitly and nothing in the public data touches it.

Why it matters: the June decision to separate the Spotify Countdown and Apple Pre-Add calls to action was a deliberate strategic choice, and we currently cannot say whether it worked. Decision 01 is also unmeasurable without it.

Who holds it: Spotify for Artists, Apple for Artists, and the lnk.to dashboard.

Gap 03 — Which playlists PTS is on

What's missing: we can see 1,207,306 of playlist reach and not what produces it. The Chartmetric current-playlists endpoint (/artist/349107/playlists/spotify/current) returns 404 for this artist.

Why it matters: it is the direct answer to the New Music Friday question. If the reach comes from a larger placement, the NMF miss is a non-event; if it comes from algorithmic surfaces, that is a different and more fragile position.

Who holds it: Spotify for Artists → playlists.

Gap 04 — Early-life data for four of the five singles

What's missing: the per-video daily series begins 15 September 2026. Summer Rain, Motherlands, Defibrillator and In Motion have a lifetime counter and nothing else.

Consequence: no age-matched YouTube comparison between the singles is possible and none has been made. The one age-matched comparison in this analysis is on the Spotify side, where the daily series reaches back to 1 January.

Fixable going forward: the series now exists. From album day onward this constraint disappears.

Gap 05 — Spotify listener segments

What's missing: super / moderate / light listener splits, and the algorithmic-versus-editorial-versus-direct source breakdown that the February internal read had.

Why it matters: Decision 03 is a test on light and moderate listeners and cannot be targeted or measured without it.

Who holds it: Spotify for Artists → Audience.

Gap 06 — The October PR and live moments

What's missing: the France24 appearance (28 Sep), the German print and online coverage, and the sold-out Barfly show have no measurable downstream signal we can isolate. They fall inside the PTS acceleration window and cannot be separated from it.

Consequence: the deck does not attribute any of the PTS acceleration to them, in either direction.

11What this analysis does not claim

  1. That the festivals caused the August expansion. The expansion is a fact and the absence of a release in the window is a fact. The causal link is plausible and unproven.
  2. That press activity on 28 September caused the PTS acceleration. The shape argues against it. We cannot see traffic sources, so the deck says what the shape is consistent with and stops.
  3. That the comment clusters represent the audience in proportion. 158 relevance-ordered comments is a sample for direction, not a measurement of share.
  4. That any single "outperformed" any other on YouTube. See Gap 04.
  5. That Spotify has withdrawn support. The NMF UK miss is real and unexplained. Every age-robust support measure we can read points the other way.
  6. That the podcast's audience will convert. It has not yet. The recommendation is to give it something to convert to, and then measure.
  7. Anything about view velocity as a quality signal. Daily gains are reported as what they are — daily gains over a stated window — and never as a ranking of how good a video is.

12Decisions, in full

Ranked by potential impact × evidence strength × ability to execute before 23 October.

Decision 01 — Put a pre-save route on every Short Confidence: High

Observation. The format that carries the campaign's only non-subscriber reach has no route to the album.

Evidence. 0 of 44 Shorts published since the album announcement carry the pre-save link. 22 have no description at all, including the album announcement and the presale announcement. 49,232 views passed through them.

Interpretation. This is not a strategy failure — the June decision to split the Spotify and Apple calls to action was right and was implemented correctly on long-form. It is an execution gap on the format that was treated as a social post rather than as a page with a description field.

Action. Description line, pinned comment and end-card on every new Short. Backfill descriptions on the 44 already published — they continue to accrue views.

Measurement. Pre-save adds attributed to YouTube in the lnk.to and Spotify for Artists dashboards, against the current baseline. Requires Gap 02 to be closed first.

Timeframe. Implement this week. Evaluate at 7 days and again on album day.

Cost. One afternoon. No spend.

Decision 02 — Publish one long-form live asset from the 2026 run Confidence: Medium

Observation. The year's largest non-launch audience expansion came from live, and none of it exists on the channel as long-form.

Evidence. Six of the ten biggest seven-day listener gains of 2026 fall in August, a month with no release (+37,315 at the peak). The channel's biggest video of all time is a live video at 1,024,903. Live demand is the largest and most international cluster in the comments. TRNSMT, Kendal Calling, YNot, Lollapalooza Berlin and the Kings of Leon support exist on the channel only as Shorts.

Interpretation. This band converts in rooms. The campaign has been publishing small-room acoustic performances (1,458–4,616 views) and has not published the thing the audience is actually asking for.

Action. One festival performance, long-form, premiered before album day. Audit what footage already exists before commissioning anything — this decision is only cheap if the footage is already shot.

Measurement. Beats the 1,458–4,616 band that every 2026 performance asset has landed in, measured at day 10. Secondary: channel 7-day views against the current 128,829.

Timeframe. Publish within 10 days. Evaluate at album day.

Confidence is Medium, not High, because the causal link between August and the festivals is unproven, and because the 2026 performance assets may be small for production reasons we cannot separate from format reasons.

Decision 03 — Paid test on light and moderate listeners, measured on followers Confidence: Medium

Observation. 587,761 people listen to this band every month and have not followed it. Album day converts people who have a standing relationship; these people do not have one.

Evidence. Followers-to-listeners fell 35.92% → 24.08% across nine months. Monthly listeners grew 7.5× faster than followers. Four of the five singles produced no follower event at all.

Interpretation. February's objection to targeting super listeners was argued then and is quantified now: the people worth paying to reach are measurably not the ones already following. The working recommendation at the time — prove engagement survives expansion before scaling spend — has now had its first half proved. Expansion happened. What has not been proved is that expansion converts.

Action. Marquee or equivalent, light and moderate segments only. Creative points at the pre-save, not at the stream.

Measurement. Follower gain per 1,000 reached, against the 41/day organic baseline. Not streams — streams are the metric that made this look fine for nine months.

Stop condition. If follower gain per 1,000 reached does not beat organic, stop. Do not scale on a stream number.

Timeframe. Live before album week. First read at 7 days.

Blocked by Gap 05 — segment targeting needs Spotify for Artists audience data.

Decision 04 — Episode two in album week, with the ask in the first thirty seconds Confidence: Medium

Observation. The campaign's highest-engagement asset brought in a new audience and asked them for nothing.

Evidence. 5.67% like rate against 0.42% on the single. 17,913 views in three days on a 41-minute video. Comments show a demonstrably non-Snuts audience arriving, several in Spanish and Portuguese. Channel subscribers moved +100 that week.

Interpretation. The format works, the guest worked, and the conversion architecture around it is missing. This is the same failure as Decision 01, at a larger scale and with a better audience.

Action. Schedule episode two into album week. Album call to action in the opening thirty seconds and the first line of the description — not buried under the social links.

Measurement. Like rate holds above 2% and the share of comments mentioning the band or the album rises against the episode-one baseline. Both conditions, not either.

Timeframe. Album week. Evaluate at 7 days.

Stop / don't start

  1. Do not buy broad paid reach. Reach is the working part. Monthly listeners +56.2%, PTS on playlists reaching 1.2 million. More top-of-funnel pours water into the same leaking bucket.
  2. Do not escalate the New Music Friday UK miss. Worth the quiet investigation Commercial has opened. Not worth a campaign-level reaction: PTS holds the campaign's highest playlist reach and highest popularity score.
  3. Do not target super listeners with paid. February's logic stands and is now measurable.
  4. Do not add more Shorts at the current cadence. 44 Shorts, 49,232 views, zero routes to the pre-save. Fix what exists before making more. Cadence is already classified as strong; volume is not the constraint.
  5. Do not read PTS as a failure. It was assessed at day three. By day ten it is 42.4% of the channel's entire trailing week and still gaining 7,700+ a day.

The one question

587,761 people listen to this band every month and have not followed it. What is the single owned mechanism putting the album in front of them before 23 October — and who owns it?
