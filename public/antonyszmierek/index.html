Signal, not proof
We can't show the extra videos caused the views. A song the label already expects to perform is also the song that gets more made for it. These ideas are also of very different ages — Stockport's video is from October 2024, Chalk's from May 2026 — so size here reflects time on the channel as much as reach. What we can say is that the ideas big enough to carry several videos are the ideas that carried the channel.

Four he already makes

What an Antony Short actually looks like

The thought
One line, held on screen, in capitals. No music video, no "out now" — just the sentence, doing what a good sentence does.
He already does this: "YOUR PERSONALITY HAS BEEN BROKEN APART AND SOLD BACK TO YOU IN A SERIES OF SHORT FORM CLIPS" · "THE HORRORS PERSIST". Leads to: the track the line comes from.
The story
He already writes these — the Flight Simulator description is one, typed out at length with no prompting. Say it to camera instead of burying it under the video.
He already does this: "the poem that became Rafters", and the Craig Cash description: "here he is performing words i wrote (!!)". Points at: the lyric video that week.
The place
An ordinary British spot, filmed plainly, with one strange observation over the top. Not "make more Stockport videos" — make more looking-at-things videos.
He already does this: "the eighth wonder of the world is in stockport x" · "Stockport or Giza?" — and the Stockport Short is the most-watched Short on the channel. Points at: any song with a place in it, and three of the five remaining titles are places.
The live moment
The spoken passages, specifically — the bit where he stops singing and talks, and the room goes with him. That is what people who have seen him write about. A phone from the side of the stage is fine.
He already does this: "Your never be alone again my love" · "everyone can face a crisis" · "rafters live at glastonbury". Live is the strongest Shorts group of the five we sorted. Points at: the album, and whatever's next live.
The bottom line
Four formats, all of them already in his catalogue. None needs a production budget.

Analysis appendix

Everything in this deck comes from public data pulled on 14 September 2026: the complete YouTube catalogue of the Antony Szmierek channel (UC-VwXR6M4HD4Wo1UpTBi4SA, 66 uploads, retrieved 16:12 UTC), 535 public comments across 21 videos (retrieved 16:38 UTC), the Watcher channel record and the Campaign Coach timeline. No YouTube Studio data is used anywhere — nothing here claims traffic sources, watch time, retention or impressions.

The one number we can't reconcile

The 66 currently public uploads sum to 702,642 views. The API reports the channel's lifetime viewCount as 1,130,576. The 427,934 difference is most likely views on uploads since made private, unlisted or removed, but the public API cannot confirm that. Because of this, no figure in this deck is expressed as a share of lifetime views. Where a share is given it is against the 702,642 total of current public uploads, and says so.

Where the recent growth actually came from

The Watcher reports +255,600 views across the channel over the 144 days it has been tracking, a window opening 23 April 2026. Sixteen uploads were published inside that window — Chalk, Seminal, Flight Simulator, all four lyric videos and nine Shorts — and their combined lifetime view total is 68,050. That is a hard ceiling: at most 27% of the growth can have come from work published in the period, and at least 73% is the back catalogue still being watched.

Observed: both figures. Interpretation: that the older videos are doing the work. It is a ceiling rather than an attribution — some of those 68,050 views also happened outside the window, which only makes the ceiling lower. This is why slide 2 says the channel is bigger than the campaign rather than crediting the campaign with the growth.

Like rate, and why slide 6 uses it

Like rate moves far less with age than view count does, so it is the one measure on which four videos of 24, 17, 11 and 4 days can be fairly set beside the rest of the channel. The long-form median across all 26 long-form uploads is 2.10%. The four post-album lyric videos are 2.76%, 3.09%, 2.64% and 4.16% — all four above it. This is not a ranking of the four against each other, which would not be sound.

Comments: how they were gathered

535 public top-level comments across 21 videos, via the API's commentThreads, ordered by YouTube's own relevance — what a visitor actually sees — with up to 40 per video. Replies are not included. One exception: Flight Simulator returned nothing under relevance and was re-pulled newest-first, giving 60; it is the only video sampled differently, and no claim in this deck rests on it alone.

Themes were found by keyword match and then read by hand. Of the 535: discovery stories 40, live 35, lyrics and words 26, comparisons to other artists 24, quoted phrases 19, place 18, the album 15, emotional impact 14, "should be bigger" 9, repeat listening 4. This is what people chose to write, not a survey. It says what the people who comment care about. It cannot say what share of the audience came from anywhere, and the deck does not claim it does.

What the campaign has released and what is booked

From the Campaign Coach. Eight moments completed 11 Mar – 11 Sep 2026, each with a hero asset confirmed live: The Heron (Mar), Chalk (May), Seminal (Jun/Jul), Flight Simulator (Aug), then the four lyric videos. Five more lyric videos are scheduled: Aussie Goldhunters 17 Sep, Godzilla Hotel 21–27 Sep, Dave's Angling Superstore 28 Sep – 4 Oct, Commune 5–11 Oct, Decoding Birdsong 12–18 Oct. The plan's own target is roughly 40–60 Shorts across the campaign with three around each release. This deck deliberately recommends fewer — one per lyric video, chosen to fit the song — on the grounds that a Short nobody wanted to make is the kind that ends up saying "out now".

Premiere and live classification

Seven uploads carry liveStreamingDetails. These are not all the same thing: Flight Simulator (2026) and Rock and a Calm Place (2023) read as premieres of studio videos, while "Working Classic (LIVE @ VIBE)" (2022) and "Giving Up For Beginners (Live at Lock 91)" (2021) are recordings of live performances. The public API does not distinguish them, so the deck makes no claim about premiere strategy.

What this deck does not know

Traffic sources, watch time, retention, impressions, click-through, whether anything was promoted, and whether any video has captions. The Watcher reports 17 of 17 videos scanned without captions — worth confirming in YouTube Studio rather than acting on from here. Playlist structure and end screens are likewise not visible from this endpoint: where the deck suggests an album playlist or a link between videos, check first whether one already exists.

A UK/IE tour exists — a Short from 8 May 2026 announces it — but no tour dates appear in any source available here, so the deck makes no claim about upcoming live activity.
