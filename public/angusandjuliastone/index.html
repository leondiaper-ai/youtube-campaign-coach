What changed

It didn't just get busier. It got a world.

Cape Forestier was four official videos, three borrowed radio sessions and a tail of promo Shorts. Karaoke Bar is seven distinct layers, each doing a different job — and the album's songs move between them.

Three times the long-form, seven layers of content — and attention is more concentrated on one video than it was last album, not less. The depth has been built. It hasn't started carrying yet.

That isn't a failure of the campaign; it is what a campaign looks like nineteen days after release, when the second and third songs have not had their moment yet. But it's the number to watch, and it's the reason the recommendations at the end are about widening the base rather than doing more of everything.

What happens next

The real test starts now.

10
Uploads in the whole of 2025

Eight were Shorts. The two long-form pieces did 1,800 views each. A channel running at ten uploads a month in April 2024 was down to one every six weeks a year later.

Karaoke Bar has solved the campaign-build problem — that is what the scorecard says, and it is not a small thing. The post-album problem is still open. The audience gathered over these five months is larger and more engaged than the one Cape Forestier gathered, and it is currently being met with the most consistent publishing this channel has ever done. Whether that survives October is the whole question.

What the team should do next

What next.

01

Keep the channel moving

No gaps over fourteen days while the album is still active. The campaign's own longest silence is sixteen; hold roughly that and the second act looks nothing like the last one.

02

Create another tentpole

Give a third album song a meaningful visual moment. Attention is sitting on one video at 42%; a third proper piece is the most direct thing in the team's control. We can't show from public data that it would broaden the audience — only that the base is currently narrow.

03

Keep filming in places

Tasmania, Byron Bay and Sugarcane Mountain are the template. Four album songs have been filmed in a room. Every one that hasn't is a room still available — and it beats another utility upload by a distance.

04

Put the catalogue back to work

Big Jet Plane opened this campaign and still holds its widest moment. Look for the next one: guests, new arrangements, live versions, a cultural opening. And start with the support gap — 21 catalogue songs holding 115M views have no lyric video, and nine exist as an audio file and nothing else.

05

Keep Shorts human

Personality, backstage, performance, the moment. Release-adjacent Shorts show no lift; the ones about the two of them do. Stop wrapping releases and keep filming the band.

Analysis

How this was measured

Sources

One pull of the channel's complete public uploads playlist — 341 videos, 5 March 2009 to 14 September 2026 — through YouTube Data API v3, plus the daily public channel readings the Watcher stores. No YouTube Studio metrics are used anywhere: no impressions, no click-through, no retention, no traffic sources, no unique viewers.

What this page does and does not claim

It does not claim that Karaoke Bar has performed better than Cape Forestier. Public lifetime view data cannot establish that — the older campaign has had roughly two additional years to accumulate views, and there is no way to age-match it.

What it claims is narrower and firmer: the 2026 campaign was run differently — more consistently, across more formats, with substantially stronger continuity around the release. Every figure in the scorecard is a count or a ratio, unaffected by how long a video has been live.

Methodology notes
Campaign windows
2024: 9 February (The Wedding Song) → 4 August, album 10 May. 2026: 29 April (Big Jet Plane live with Dua Lipa) → 14 September, album 4 September. The 2026 window opens on the Dua Lipa performance because it is the campaign's first asset, a week before the first album single.
Format classification
From title text. "Official Music Video" / "Official Video" → official video; "Lyric Video" → lyric; "Instrumental Karaoke" → karaoke; live, acoustic, session or a named venue → live/acoustic; anything under 62 seconds → Short. The rule is stated so the figures can be re-derived.
Medians, not averages
Format comparisons use medians. One official video at 1.1M would otherwise drag every average it touched.
Public subscribers
Displayed subscriber counts are rounded by YouTube, so movement is given as observed readings rather than as a precise figure or a growth multiple.
Attribution
Public data shows co-occurrence, not asset-level causation. Release-week channel movement is not credited to any individual upload.
Karaoke and instrumental versions
These are counted as campaign uploads because they are, but they are catalogue-completeness assets rather than reach assets, and are described that way. Their inclusion is what moves the campaign's median long-form figure, which is why medians by format are shown instead.
