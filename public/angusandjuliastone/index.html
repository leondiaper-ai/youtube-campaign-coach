The part worth not repeating

Last album, the channel went quiet for a year.

Eight of those ten were Shorts. The channel published two long-form videos in twelve months, and neither reached 2,000 views. A campaign that had been running at ten uploads a month in April 2024 was down to one every six weeks by the following spring.

That is the risk this campaign has earned the right to avoid. The audience it has just gathered is larger than the one Cape Forestier gathered, and it is currently being met with the most consistent publishing the channel has ever done. The question is only whether that survives October.

Post-album

Five things to keep doing.

Each of these is already in the campaign and already working on this channel. None of them requires a new idea — only that they continue past release week.

01 · The strongest evidence on the page

Keep filming songs in places.

A performance in a named room is this channel's most reliable format after the official video — a 39.8K median against 16.3K for a lyric video. Four album songs have been filmed in a room so far. Every one that has not is a room still available.

02 · The gap that opened last time

Don't let a fortnight pass.

The campaign's longest silence so far is sixteen days. Holding roughly that — something every two weeks, whatever size — is the difference between this album's second act and the last one's.

03 · Already proven this year

Put the catalogue back to work.

The campaign's widest moment was Big Jet Plane with a guest — a 2010 song. Chateau, Grizzly Bear and A Heartbreak are the channel's biggest videos and are untouched by this campaign. A new live version of an old song is a new asset for a new audience.

04 · The concentration problem

Give a third song a real video.

Two official videos carry 42% and 20% of campaign views. Every album since has one or two songs that grow late — Monroe and The Cherry Farm both have performance films and audiences already. A third proper video is the most direct way to widen the base.

05 · Where the Shorts actually landed

Keep Shorts personal, not promotional.

Shorts near a release did no better than Shorts anywhere else — 3,885 against 3,641. The ones that travelled were the collaboration, the wrong chord, the fifteen seconds off duty. The channel's Shorts median has risen from 2.6K to 3.9K by being about the two of them.

And one to simply continue

Say the next date out loud.

The release date was announced by the band, to camera, five days out. Tour dates, a film, a deluxe — whatever comes next, that is the move that already works here.

Every figure above is from the campaign's own uploads, not from a benchmark. The comparisons are the channel against itself.

Methodology notes
Campaign windows
2024: 9 February (The Wedding Song) → 4 August, album 10 May. 2026: 29 April (Big Jet Plane live with Dua Lipa) → 14 September, album 4 September. The 2026 window opens on the Dua Lipa performance because it is the campaign's first asset, a week before the first album single.
Format classification
From title text. "Official Music Video" / "Official Video" → official video; "Lyric Video" → lyric; "Instrumental Karaoke" → karaoke; live, acoustic, session or a named venue → live/acoustic; anything under 62 seconds → Short. The rule is stated so the figures can be re-derived.
Medians, not averages
Format comparisons use medians. One official video at 1.1M would otherwise drag every average it touched.
Public subscribers
Displayed subscriber counts are rounded by YouTube, so movement is given as observed readings rather than as a precise figure or a growth multiple.
Attribution
Public data shows co-occurrence, not asset-level causation. Release-week channel movement is not credited to any individual upload.
Karaoke and instrumental versions
These are counted as campaign uploads because they are, but they are catalogue-completeness assets rather than reach assets, and are described that way. Their inclusion is what moves the campaign's median long-form figure, which is why medians by format are shown instead.
