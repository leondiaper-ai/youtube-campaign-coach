Who this is, verified

A producer whose audience
argues about production.

underscores is April Harper Grey — American singer, songwriter and producer, working solo. She uses she/her. The styling is lower-case and she is not a band.

Three albums: Fishmonger (2021), Wallsocket (2023) — a concept record about small-town America promoted with an alternate-reality game — and U, released 20 March 2026 on Mom + Pop. Pitchfork Best New Music, five stars in NME. She opened for Charli XCX in autumn 2026.

That history matters for one reason: the world-building is already how she works. Nothing in this deck asks her to become a different kind of artist.

underscores — Topic

Job: repeat listening. 13,797,333 lifetime views against 549 subscribers — the signature of a passive surface. No visual identity, no community, no route back to the artist channel. The largest single finding on this slide.

03 · Collaborative

Features and remixes

Job: lateral discovery. Seven collaborators across the catalogue — Jane Remover, gabby start, henhouse!, 8485, Knapsack, and in this era Yves and Frost Children. The Yves remix drew 726,794, comparable to a lead single. Remix as discovery is demonstrated here, not theoretical.

04 · Third-party

Fan and community uploads

Job: cultural reach. Visible in the comments we sampled — a running joke travels between videos, so people are moving across the catalogue.

Not quantified. No systematic search was run. No third-party view is owned, controlled or monetised by the artist, and none is counted anywhere in this deck.

Avoiding double-counting

On 20 March 2026 ten uploads landed in one day: nine track-level (♫) audio uploads plus a 34-minute album video. Those nine are the same recordings Topic also carries. A listener who plays Lovefield on Topic and watches the (♫) upload is one person consuming one song on two surfaces. They stay in separate columns throughout.

So what

Make this the default shape for 2027. It is cheap, repeatable, and already hers.

The honest limit on this slide

This is a sequence, not a funnel. We know the Shorts preceded the video and the video performed. We do not know that anyone crossed from one to the other — that needs traffic-source data, which is the first analytics ask on slide 11.

These are not age-matched

The Peace has had 65 days; Music has had 476. No per-video daily history exists that far back, so we cannot say what Music had at day 65. The comparison is a floor: The Peace has reached 73% of Music's lifetime total in 14% of the time, and the true pace gap is wider than that, not narrower.

The counter-case, stated

Music and Do It carry larger lifetime totals. If the goal were maximum catalogue reach rather than current-moment momentum, Music is the bigger door. For a November activation tied to a live run, The Peace is the correct lead.

Honest about the weakest link

The Shorts→video journey is the part we cannot evidence. Treat the Shorts as worth doing because they are cheap and the format engages — 5.84% median like rate against 2.56% long-form — not because a conversion rate has been demonstrated.

Deliberately not recommended

  • New lyric videos. 18 exist, median 111,637, and the (♫) uploads cover this record. No distinct audience need.
  • A visualiser for The Peace. It has a video, a remix and an audio upload. A fourth surface for one song splits attention.
  • Changing the title grammar. It is legible and era-specific. Standardising would cost more than it returns.

What we can do ourselves,
and what we'd need help with.

Management can action directly

  • Restart publishing before 10 November. No permission required, no new shoot.
  • Capture one UK show and the pop-up. Crew already present.
  • Commission vertical at every future shoot as standard.
  • Confirm Official Artist Channel configuration with the distributor, so Topic consumption consolidates correctly.

Requires YouTube involvement

  • Analytics access. UK viewing share, traffic sources, returning viewers, retention on the long-form streams, subscriber gain by video — everything this deck had to leave open.
  • Is a UK Foundry moment possible around the November run? YouTube's own 2026 Foundry activity included a UK release party with Natanya; that is the precedent we point at, not an expectation.
  • Editorial consideration for a London live asset, if one is captured.
  • A view on process-led long-form. Our data says it over-performs here; YouTube can say whether that holds across comparable artists.

The three questions worth asking in the room

  • Does the Short→video journey exist here at all? We have run the sequence twice and cannot see whether it works. One traffic-source read settles how the 2027 campaign is built.
  • Do the UK ticket buyers watch the channel? Four sold-out shows, no UK viewing data.
  • Is 3.4 hours of production footage an anomaly or a format? It out-performed every official video on like rate. YouTube sees comparable artists; we see one channel.

Material limitations

  • 36.6% view gap. Channel reports 36,814,369 lifetime; the 102 public uploads sum to 23,330,281. The 13,484,088 difference is deleted, private or unlisted. No figure here is a share of lifetime.
  • No Studio metrics. No impressions, CTR, retention, traffic sources, unique viewers, territory splits or subscriber-gain-by-video.
  • No age-matched comparison. No per-video daily history exists for the older singles, so pace comparisons are floors.
  • Comment sample is relevance-ordered, n=160 across four videos. Not random.
  • Third-party uploads unmapped. No systematic search was run.
  • BBC pundits page inaccessible. Year, names and roles unverified; no individual named.
  • Snapshot. Newest upload 28 Aug 2026. Figures move.
