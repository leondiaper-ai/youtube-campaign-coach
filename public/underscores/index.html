What we observe

  • The Peace is the strongest YouTube entry point — and it is the one the streaming plan already favours, which is a useful alignment rather than a coincidence to assume. 1,373,924 views in 65 days, a 5.25% like rate and 3,089 comments: the highest engagement rate of any single in the era.
  • Release sequencing already works. Do It ran Shorts → video → alternate version across 25 days. The video is the channel's second-biggest current-era upload.
  • A 3.4-hour production stream drew 136,839 views. That is not a normal outcome for long-form process content and it points at a real, underexploited appetite.
  • The Topic channel holds 13.8M views on 549 subscribers. Catalogue consumption is happening on a surface with no visual identity and no path back to the artist channel.

What we do not know

  • Whether any Short sent anyone to any video. We have no traffic-source data — every sequence in this review is co-occurrence, not causation.
  • What share of viewing is UK. Four of five UK shows sold out, which is live demand; it is not evidence those buyers watch the channel.
  • Retention, returning viewers, subscriber gain by video, impressions, click-through. All Studio metrics. All listed as asks rather than guessed.
  • Where 13,484,088 lifetime views went. The channel total exceeds the sum of its 102 public videos by 36.6% — deleted, private or unlisted. Nothing here is expressed as a share of lifetime.

02_Artist and the UK opportunity

A solo producer-songwriter with a verified
platform relationship and sold-out UK demand

underscores is April Harper Grey — an American singer, songwriter and producer, working solo. She uses she/her pronouns. The styling is lower-case: underscores, never capitalised, and not a band.

Three albums: Fishmonger (2021), Wallsocket (2023) — a concept record about small-town America promoted with an alternate-reality game — and U, released 20 March 2026 on Mom + Pop, which took Pitchfork's Best New Music and five stars from NME. She opened for Charli XCX in autumn 2026.

That trajectory matters here for one reason: the ARG, the concept record and the world-building are already how this artist works. Nothing in this review asks her to become a different kind of creator. It asks whether YouTube is set up to hold what she already makes.

Gap — not quantified

We have not systematically mapped third-party uploads. Doing so needs a search-and-verify pass we have not run. No third-party view is owned, controlled or monetised by the artist, and none is counted anywhere in this review.

Avoiding double-counting

On 20 March 2026 ten uploads landed in one day: nine track-level audio uploads marked (♫) plus a full album video. Those nine are the same recordings the Topic channel also carries. A listener who plays Lovefield on Topic and watches the (♫) upload is one person consuming one song on two surfaces. Throughout this review they stay in separate columns.

The 2027 campaign should treat this as the default shape. We cannot claim the Shorts drove the video — no traffic-source data — but the shape is repeatable and cheap, and it is already this artist's own.

06_Creative strengths and best practice

A channel organised by someone
who thinks in worlds, not uploads

Separating two things the rest of this document keeps apart: interesting creative choices and demonstrated performance drivers. Both are worth preserving. Only the second is evidence.

Demonstrated — supported by the numbers

1. The alternate version as a second release

Do It shipped three times: official video (1,477,883), Choreo Ver. (415,363) and Yves Remix (726,794). Music shipped twice: video (1,871,328) and DDR Ver. (61,773).

The pattern is not uniform — DDR Ver. did a thirtieth of Choreo Ver. Alternate versions work when they add a performance or a collaborator, not when they only re-skin the audio.

2. Process content as a format in its own right

The A-side production stream has a 5.97% like rate across 3.4 hours — higher than any official video on the channel. The comment sample backs this up: "drumless production going crazy" is the second most-liked comment we collected, at 2,858 likes.

3. Complete catalogue surfacing

Nine track-level (♫) uploads on album day, plus 18 lyric videos across earlier eras (median 111,637 views). Every song has a home on the channel. That is unusual at this scale and it is why catalogue keeps earning.

Distinctive — creative signature, not yet measurable

Typographic discipline

Titles follow a strict grammar: underscores — Song for videos, Song ‎ (♫) for audio, 《live from VENUE》 for live. Era punctuation changes between records — straight quotes for Wallsocket, em-dashes for U.

A viewer can tell what a thing is and which era it belongs to from the title alone. Most artist channels cannot say that. Worth protecting rather than standardising away.

World-building as release strategy

Wallsocket (2023) was a concept record about small-town America promoted with an alternate-reality game. U was written, in the artist's framing, for malls, airports and supermarkets.

YouTube's own Foundry citation names "world-building visuals" as the reason she was selected. The platform has already told us what it values here.

The channel is older than the project

Opened July 2013. Its single most-viewed upload remains a 2014 dubstep production tutorial ↗ at 2,143,040 views — made years before underscores existed as a project.

We flag this rather than recommend anything. It skews any "top video" view of the channel, and whether it still belongs on the front of the channel is an artist decision, not a strategy one.

Four things the evidence points at

01Highest confidence

Why it matters: 2025 showed what cadence does here — 11 Shorts and the channel's best year. 2026 has four Shorts in eight months.

03Proven, under-used

Observe: 3.4-hour stream, 136,839 views, 5.97% like rate — above every official video.

Why it matters: the audience discusses production unprompted. The format is cheap, it is already hers, and it is the one thing on the channel with no competitor doing it better.

04Absent entirely

No UK content, and no live content since August

Observe: one live upload in the current era, filmed in Chicago. Nothing UK-specific anywhere on the channel.

Why it matters: four of five UK shows sold out. Whether that audience overlaps with the channel audience is unknown — and will stay unknown unless something exists for them to find.

Do: capture November. Even minimally.

10_Building toward the 2027 album

What should be settled before
the next campaign starts

Album timing, budget and rollout are unconfirmed. So this is a list of things worth establishing during the tour window, when the cost of learning is low.

Formats worth repeating

  • The Do It ladder — Shorts, video, alternate version, remix. Six assets from one song.
  • Process film as a recurring strand, not album-week only.
  • Remix with a named collaborator — Yves drew 726,794, comparable to a lead single.
  • Track-level audio on release day, continuing the (♫) convention.

Filming requirements

  • Capture process during the making of the record, not reconstructed afterwards. The existing streams work because they are unperformed.
  • Vertical at the shoot, so Shorts are a by-product rather than a separate commission.
  • One properly captured live show per territory cycle.

Measurement to put in place now

  • Baseline UK viewing share before November, so the tour window can be read against something.
  • Traffic sources on one Short→video pair, to settle the question this review cannot.
  • Returning-viewer rate across the era, to test whether the running-joke behaviour is as widespread as it looks.

Foundry and the tour as a learning window

underscores is a verified Foundry Class of 2026 artist. YouTube's published description of Foundry activity in 2026 includes music video screening events (Julia Wolf, Cherry Lane Theatre), release parties (Kelela in NYC; Natanya in the UK) and the Avenues performance series.

Those are documented precedents, and the UK one is directly relevant. We are not assuming any of them is available here — what Foundry provides to a given artist is decided by YouTube, and section 11 frames these as questions rather than expectations.

11_YouTube UK — discussion points

Separating what we can do
from what we would need help with

Management can action directly

  • Restart publishing before 10 November. No permission required, no new shoot.
  • Capture one UK show and the pop-up. Crew already present.
  • Commission vertical at every future shoot as standard.
  • Confirm Official Artist Channel configuration with the distributor, so Topic consumption consolidates correctly.

Requires YouTube involvement

  • Analytics access. UK viewing share, traffic sources, returning viewers, retention on the long-form streams, subscriber gain by video. Everything this review had to leave open.
  • Is a UK Foundry moment possible around the November run? The Natanya UK release party is the precedent we are pointing at.
  • Editorial consideration for the live asset, if one is captured in London.
  • A view on process-led long-form. Our data says it over-performs here; YouTube can say whether that holds across comparable artists.

The three questions worth asking in the room

  1. 1. Does the Short→video journey exist here at all? We have run the sequence twice and cannot see whether it works. One traffic-source read settles how the 2027 campaign is built.
  2. 2. Do the UK ticket buyers watch the channel? Four sold-out shows and no UK viewing data. If the overlap is small, that is an acquisition opportunity, not a failure.
  3. 3. Is 3.4 hours of production footage an anomaly or a format? It out-performed every official video on like rate. YouTube sees comparable artists; we see one channel.
