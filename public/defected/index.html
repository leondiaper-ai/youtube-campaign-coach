Worth protecting

Six things already working.

01

The Friday show

52 episodes a year since 2019. The weekly appointment, with artwork that reads at a glance. Keep the slot and the colours; bring the episodes together (next).

02

The DFTD artwork

Three million-view records in 2025. Artwork that says “Defected release” before the title is read. Worth using on every release upload.

03

The HQ sets

59 sets, nine over 100K. The studio series keeps the sets coming between seasons and has produced more big ones than any event. Keep it going.

04

The catalogue mixes

16 of 30 passed 100K in 2022–25. The cheapest large audience the channel has. Worth a regular calendar.

05

The Premieres

189 Premieres, used for the right things: every podcast episode, every music video, most event sets, with chat replay left on. The audio uploads and the radio show, rightly, aren't premiered.

06

The co-publishing

18 videos on Home carry a co-publisher — artists, and the venue Chinois Ibiza — so YouTube recommends each to both audiences. A habit worth keeping and extending to catalogue pieces with the original artists.

The suggestions that follow don't ask for a new content line. They're about making what already exists easier to find, and giving a few of the label's own records and moments more room.

Defected has plenty of music knowledge and stories to draw on, and the Shorts that use it — a classic record, the story of a sample, Do You Remember House? — have typically reached about twice the audience of announcement Shorts. That's 22 clips, so it's a promising sign rather than a rule. Worth a small run alongside the existing promotional clips: one slot a week (the “house classic for your Monday” slot already exists), eight to twelve weeks, five kinds of clip from material the label already has:

  • Do You Remember House? · one record, one memory → links to the record
  • Story of the Sample · 30 seconds of music history → links to the original
  • The moment · a crowd reaction from a Malta or Pacha set → links to the full set
  • The story · a 40-second podcast moment → links to the episode
  • This Friday · the week's pick from the show → links to the radio episode
Each Short can carry YouTube's related-video link to the full set, episode or record (set in Studio; worth checking the option is available on the channel). Whether Shorts viewers go on to watch the longer video is the thing to find out, not something to assume.
Public YouTube data · 198 Shorts, 1 Jan 2025 → 16 Oct 2026, grouped by title · the channel's four biggest Shorts ever (14.4M, 6.6M, 3.3M, 1.5M, 2022–23) are reposted crowd and dance clips and sit outside all three groups.
Worth trying · 6

Give each podcast episode more moments to reach fans.

Jan 2024Moments in Music, season one · nine episodes in 25 monthsFeb 2026

Season one already has the things that matter: a branded cover, its own playlist, a Home shelf, and guests with stories to tell. Each episode was announced with a Short, but only one clip from inside a conversation was published — Skream on the track that started it all — and it reached more viewers (14.3K) than any announcement. Nine episodes over 25 months, with gaps of up to seven months; views 2.4K–31K, typically 17K.

The label's planned season two isn't announced publicly yet, so no date is given here. These suggestions are about how episodes are cut and released, whenever that is.
14.3KSkream · the track that started it all
  1. Give each episode a small set of standalone clips, each built around one strong story or quote and linked to the episode. The Skream clip is the model; the announcement becomes one of four, not the only one.
  2. A regular slot for the season, released as a run, with the playlist and the Podcasts tab updated on day one.
  3. Episode artwork with the guest's face and one line — the Sam Divine episode already does this — and chapters in every description.
  4. Link the guest's music both ways. The episode points to the guest's Defected releases or set, and those point back to the episode.
How we'd know: episode views at 28 days against the season-one typical (17K); clip views against the announcements (about 4K); related-link clicks in Studio.
Public YouTube data · S1E01 22 Jan 2024 → S1E09 18 Feb 2026 · Apple Podcasts and Spotify list the same nine episodes as at 16 Oct 2026.
In summary

Three opportunities to build on a strong channel.

01

Bring the radio episodes together

An up-to-date playlist, recognisable artwork and a clear place on Home.

02

Build selected music moments

Give a flagship Premiere extra support, or explore a catalogue idea alongside existing plans.

03

Share more of the stories

Test clips from artist conversations, podcast episodes and the history behind key records.

Defected already does a great deal well. These are a few practical ideas to explore together.

Claim boundary. Whether a trailer ran before a Premiere, how many reminders were taken, concurrent viewers and chat volume are not public; a trailer is not shown after the Premiere ends, so its absence now says nothing about whether one was used. The slide therefore describes what the channel visibly does (premieres the right formats, announces with Shorts, leaves chat replay on) and offers three possibilities without claiming any is missing. Lifetime views on a premiered video are not a Premiere audience. How we'd know: reminders set, concurrent viewers and chat messages in Studio for a Premiere run this way, against the channel's usual set Premieres.

Slide 14 · the summary, supporting detail

The closing slide keeps three headline opportunities. The working behind each, as developed earlier in the review, is kept here.

01 · Bring the radio episodes together
A playlist of every episode, set as a podcast, with the show's artwork and a shelf on Home. Supporting steps: the tracklist kept, renamed, with a link to the playlist in every episode description; the sets shelf renamed for what it holds, with Croatia, Malta and Shorts shelves added; one title format for the HQ sets. How we'd know: playlist views, and episode views arriving from it, after 28 days; Home-tab impressions and clicks by shelf.
02 · Build selected music moments
One selected Premiere with a bigger moment around it; a lyric video explored for Cola, subject to partnerships and artist plans; a catalogue-milestone collection discussed alongside the label's current plans. Supporting steps: trailer and live-redirect eligibility confirmed separately in Studio before the Premiere; tracklist and chapters on the selected sets; catalogue mixes on a calendar with one title format. How we'd know: reminders, concurrent viewers and chat in Studio against the usual Premieres; a lyric video's views and sources at 30 days; collection playlist views.
03 · Share more of the stories
Story-led Shorts tested over a set period; a few clips from inside each podcast episode when season two comes. Supporting steps: each clip linked to the full set, episode or record; podcast artwork led by the guest, with the guest's music linked both ways; the announcement becomes one clip among several, not the only one. How we'd know: typical views and related-link clicks by kind of clip; episode views at 28 days against 17K; clip views against the 4K the announcements reached.

Questions only YouTube Studio can answer

  • Traffic sources for the radio show: search, browse, external, playlist.
  • Reminders set, concurrent viewers and chat volume on past Premieres; whether trailers were used.
  • Impressions and click-through for HQ sets against event sets, over the same period.
  • Search terms bringing people to the catalogue uploads; traffic from Shorts audio.
  • Related-link clicks on the few Shorts that already carry one.

What we'll be able to say later

  • We started recording the channel's public figures daily on 16 October 2026. From mid-November we can show how each new upload performs in its first week; from mid-January, quarter against quarter.
  • Then: radio episode views at 7 and 28 days by host; Shorts by kind of clip at 7 days; the anniversary playlist and any lyric video at 30 and 90 days, against the figures in this deck.
  • Whether the back catalogue is still growing, and where, can only be read from that record — nothing in this deck claims it.

Method

Sources
Public YouTube Data API v3 through the project's own routes (uploads, playlists, channel sections); the live channel in a browser on 16 October 2026; Apple Podcasts and Spotify for the podcast listing. No YouTube Studio data of any kind.
Comparison rule
No lifetime-view ranking across upload ages. Series are compared within the same window or by structural measures: cadence, consistency, format mix, share passing a threshold. Where a comparison cannot be made fairly the figures are stated with their limits.
Attribution
Public data shows co-occurrence. No result is attributed to a setting, a title, a thumbnail or a host. Shorts-to-long-form conversion is a hypothesis for the test on slide 12.
Snapshots
The channel entered daily public snapshots on 16 October 2026. Until there is history, nothing here describes momentum, release-week shape or catalogue growth. The follow-up plan is under Slide 14 above.
Not retrieved
The 243 oldest uploads (2009 to mid-2011); the Live and Posts tabs; end screens, cards and Community posts, which the public API does not expose.
