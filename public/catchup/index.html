Watcher — what's changed

Taken from the commit history, not memory.
Everything below shipped in the last four weeks.
New9

Per-video daily observations

Views, likes and comments captured per video per day. The first time we can read a trajectory instead of a lifetime counter — it's what caught the Snuts single accelerating six-fold after we'd already written it off at day three.

2 Oct · /api/video-series · series starts mid-Sept, no backfill

Readings for videos we don't own

One live reading for any public video, including channels outside the roster — a festival's upload, a broadcaster's performance, a label-side asset. Those were previously invisible to us.

2 Oct · /api/video-stats

Chartmetric integration

Territories, plus Spotify, TikTok, Shazam and Instagram series with a date window. Lets us test whether a YouTube moment shows up anywhere else — and, on the Snuts, whether a Spotify moment showed up on YouTube. It didn't.

25 Sep, extended 2 Oct · UK Landscape uses it

Shorts vs long-form split

Separated across the roster and mounted on the dashboard, with a coverage confidence label. We deliberately refuse to extrapolate a channel-level split the API cannot give us.

30 Sep · /api/format-split

Comments as audience research

Verbatim comments read at scale, with author and link destinations. Freak Slug's tour demand came out of this, not out of a metric — 29 people asking for dates, naming 16 cities.

14 Sep, extended 5 Oct · /api/comment-scan

Fan response on every artist page

Shows one real fan comment with its like count, or shows nothing. Replaced generated copy like "strong fan response". Rule-based, so it's auditable.

16 Sep

UK YouTube Landscape

Three rankings that are allowed to disagree — UK consumption, crossover, biggest UK audience — never combined into one score. Built to be shareable outside Virgin.

25 Sep · Open

Foundry Watch

Both 2026 Foundry cohorts, 40 channels, measured through the same pipeline as everything else so the numbers are comparable by construction.

18 Sep · Open

Team and regional boards

Australia and Nordics boards live. Artists are shared across teams and sync the same night; boards, pins and notes stay private to each team.

23 Sep · Team Watcher
Improved8

Recent views lead, lifetime demoted

Probably the single most useful change. A ten-year-old catalogue no longer drowns this week's release on the artist page, and video grids rank by what's actually moving.

30 Sep

One artist page for everyone

Watcher, team boards and the preview route now render the same page rather than three lookalikes. Channel Behaviour became a proper page with viewing separated from publishing.

23 + 30 Sep · Watcher

The daily collection stopped failing silently

A sync outage froze "last sync" at 14 September while the job died every morning for four days. Now batched, resumable, and it writes an honest cursor when it stops early rather than nothing.

18 + 23 Sep · the most important fix in the window

Format writes are now counted

They had been failing with nothing but a console warning, so a pipeline writing nothing looked identical to one that worked. The cron now reports its own outcomes.

30 Sep

Campaign covers age correctly

A teaser no longer outranks the thing it was teasing, and the cover leads on the release that's actually out. Fixed after a CHVRCHES headline went stale mid-campaign.

23 Sep – 5 Oct

Format classification fixes

Cold Blue Dawn was being read as a live performance; My Whole World wasn't being read as a music video at all. Both now carry human overrides where the title has no format marker.

30 Sep

Chartmetric identity, twice burned

Bleachers was mapped to an Australian folk artist. A cached miss on K-Trap had been stored as permanent, so he looked absent when he wasn't. Misses now expire; human-verified pins override.

25 + 30 Sep

Descriptions no longer truncated

The catalogue route capped descriptions at 400 characters, which meant a CTA audit couldn't tell a missing link from a cut-off one. It had already produced a wrong answer on the Snuts.

04

What we're starting to learn

Patterns across channels, with the artist
work as evidence underneath.
Correction · worth knowing before anyone quotes it

Our "22 of 23 channels had no follow-up" figure doesn't exist.

We went looking for the source. 22 of 23 is real — but it measures Shorts adoption in a curated campaign set, not follow-up. The gap itself is genuine in individual deep dives. It has never been measured across the roster, and the 7–14 day boundary is our own convention, not something YouTube publishes.

22 of 23 (95.7%) — campaigns including at least one Short
131 of 136 (96.3%) — campaigns recording zero return moments. This is the real cross-campaign number.
The roster-wide follow-up test exists in code and has never been run.
Finding 01

Post-release follow-through is inconsistent, and we can't yet prove it costs anything.

We keep finding the gap artist by artist. What we can't do is show that closing it changes an outcome — reverse causation is wide open, because teams add follow-ups to releases that are already working.

CHVRCHES · 0 of 4 Screen Violence heroes had anything meaningful in the 7–14 day window. Gaps of 41, 39, 22 and 6 days.
Kings of Leon · 6 of 11 did, median gap 8 days — the counter-example.
Tove Lo · 43 days silent, then 12 uploads on album day, then nothing for 17.
Angus & Julia Stone · the 2024 silence began 17 days after the album.
Finding 02

Campaign viewing concentrates into two or three assets, almost everywhere.

Not a Shorts story or a format story — a shape-of-the-distribution story. The practical consequence is that supporting a release evenly is the wrong default, and we've started saying so in the recommendations.

Freak Slug · 3 of 46 uploads = 72.7% of window views. One Short did 12,041; the next did 4,709.
Tove Lo · one track = 59.7% of the album-day set, 14.3× the median of the other ten.
Angus & Julia Stone · top asset went 35% → 42% across two campaigns.
Freak Slug · Tove Lo
Finding 03

Catalogue can dominate a channel completely — including during a campaign.

This changed how we scope a deep dive. If most viewing is historic, a campaign read that only looks at campaign uploads is answering a small question loudly.

Bleachers · 97.3% of 23.9M views over 154 days landed on videos published before the window — with an album released inside it.
CHVRCHES · 74,266 views a day across 79 days with nothing published and no upload for 340 days.
Kings of Leon · 86.6% of visible viewing comes from uploads made 2008–13.
Angus & Julia Stone · one 2017 song still takes 13,679 views a day.
Finding 04

Shorts and long-form have to be analysed separately — and we cannot see between them.

We've made this a hard internal rule rather than implying a funnel. We can count Shorts, date them and measure their views. We cannot observe a Shorts viewer going on to watch long-form. That limit shapes what we're willing to recommend.

55.1% of 136 campaigns include at least one Short. Top quartile 82.4%, bottom quartile 34.3%.
CHVRCHES · 38 Shorts = 0.4% of visible views.
The Snuts · 44 Shorts, median 1,088 views, 39 comments in total against 30,200 subscribers.
Freak Slug · Shorts are 90% of uploads and 21% of views.
Finding 05

Release assets do very different jobs, and we've been treating them as one thing.

Reach and engagement frequently run in opposite directions on the same channel. The highest-reach asset is often the one nobody engages with, and the thing people actually respond to is small.

The Snuts · above 700 views/day, like rate 0.31–0.45%. Below it, 2.2–8.1%. The podcast reached non-fans at a 5.66% like rate — and carried no link at all.
Freak Slug · visualiser 399 views/day vs BTS 10/day. Comparable ages, very different value.
VENUS GRRRLS · world content reaches 3× further; announcements get liked more by people already there.
Finding 06

Benchmarks almost certainly need to be per artist type, not per roster.

Our one segmentation that works today is a four-state health classification. Everything we compare across the whole roster mixes a legacy act with 2 billion lifetime views and a developing artist with 3,000 subscribers, and calls the result a benchmark.

Shorts adoption by tier · Mid 72.4% · Large 57.9% · Developing 32.3%. The gap is at the small end, and a roster average hides it.
Live session adoption · Virgin 63.4% vs market 78.6% — our largest format gap, but unstratified.
Of 30 channels classified growing, only 7 were up on views week-on-week. One week only — a flag, not a finding.

06

Where the Watcher is going

The thing most worth your time today.
Direction · for discussion, not a result

From channel monitoring to a behavioural intelligence layer.

We monitor enough channels now that the useful question has changed. It isn't "how is this artist doing" any more — it's "what do campaigns that look like this one normally do?"

To answer that we'd need to tag every channel, then compare campaigns within a bucket rather than across the whole roster. Right now every benchmark we publish averages a legacy act and a developing artist together.

"Artists should post more on YouTube."
"For UK indie artists this size, campaigns with a second meaningful asset inside two weeks hold viewing longer than campaigns without one."

We are nowhere near the second statement — and we'd rather have the approach pulled apart now than after we've built on it.

What we'd tag on
Artist size Upload frequency Shorts / long-form mix Campaign behaviour Genre Career stage Catalogue vs frontline Release type Territory

Solid = we hold it today. Dashed = we don't. Genre covers 16% of the roster and sits in the wrong layer; career stage and catalogue-vs-frontline don't exist at all.

Why this is the right moment

Per-video daily history started mid-September. In a few months we'll have enough behavioural series to test whether a bucket behaves differently — which is the first time any of this becomes answerable rather than assertable.

Things to discuss with you

Where YouTube has data, context or product
knowledge we don't.
Segmentation

If you were building our bucketing, what would you tag on — and what would you throw out?

We'd rather hear that genre is a red herring now than in six months.

Data access

What's realistically shareable from Studio, and at what level?

Most of our open questions are one field away. Traffic sources, returning viewers, catalogue-to-new-release overlap. Aggregate would do.

Shorts

What do you actually see between Shorts and long-form music consumption?

We've banned ourselves from claiming a funnel we can't observe. You can observe it.

Follow-up

Does structured post-release activity affect recommendation longevity?

We keep finding the gap and can't test whether closing it does anything.

Collabs

How should we evaluate a collaboration when we don't own both sides of the analytics?

HEARTSTEEL is the live example — a game publisher's channel and four featured artists.

Benchmarks

Are there benchmarks you could share by artist size, genre or format?

Even coarse ranges would stop us building our own from 185 channels and calling it a market view.

Supply

Are average views per video falling as consumption grows?

More of our roster is declining week-on-week than growing, while cadence holds. We can't separate cyclical from structural.

API

Two API things that cost us accuracy.

Uploads playlists return more items than the channel's own public video count, and descriptions truncate. Both have produced wrong answers we had to catch late.

Products

Stations and CTV — what's real, and who's eligible?

Two artists this quarter had a case for a programmed channel. We stopped short of recommending it.
