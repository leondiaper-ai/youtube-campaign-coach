Create Music Group × YouTube · exploratory review
VMG UK · Exploratory review · 14 October 2026

Create Music Group
on YouTube

Findings from Create’s YouTube channel ecosystem: what we observed, where channels differ, what the examples suggest, and what remains unknown.

Research to inform discussion, not a strategy recommendation. Public YouTube data read on 14 Oct 2026: every public upload on the 23 channels in the reviewed groupings (39,215), a three-year sample of 19 channels (8,687 uploads) and 12 featured-artist channels.

Findings in brief

Four things the public data shows.

1

Recent results differ widely, whatever the subscriber count

A typical Trap Nation track upload has 44K views (127 uploads, 3–18 months old). House Nation, with 3.9M subscribers, has 5.6K (147 uploads).

2

Lifetime views are concentrated

39,215 public uploads on 23 channels hold 32.1B lifetime views. In The Nations, the top 185 of 18,507 uploads hold 64% of the group’s lifetime views, and Trap Nation alone holds 65%.

3

Older uploads appear to keep gaining views

Trap Nation grew by 467.1M views between 6 Feb 2025 and 14 Oct 2026. Everything it published in that time holds 11.5M today.

4

In four artist cases, the artist’s channel had the larger visible results

bbno$’s typical upload has 2.1M views; typical network placements of the same age have 6.1K. DeeBaby, Marshmello and Jason Derulo point the same way, with fewer comparable placements.

Not visible publicly: whether viewers return, where views come from, and what any of this earns. Modest views relative to subscribers don’t establish poor economics or an inactive audience.

YouTube features by channel type · three-year sample

Label channels link Shorts to their releases. Curators organise playlists by listening occasion.

Shorts linked to a full track

Monstercat Uncaged: 44 of 68 Shorts link to its own release; Silk 13 of 21; Instinct 8 of 14. Chill Nation 0 of 35; House Nation 1 of 67.

Playlists as programming

Chill Nation artist “weeks”; House Nation “Car Music” (175 videos) and “Summer House” (253); Monstercat volume compilations; Aureal translates whole albums.

Long listening formats

Monstercat “Best of” album mixes (Best of 2023: 91 minutes, 293K views); Silk “24/7 Live Radio”. None seen on Trap or Chill Nation in the sample.

Packaging

Every Nations channel uses one template, logo over landscape. Monstercat badges each imprint. 7clouds and Aureal lead with lyric typography.

Artist channel and network · what public data can and can’t separate

For bbno$, DeeBaby and Marshmello, visible results sit mainly on the artist’s channel. Network placements add volume with smaller visible results.

Artist channels In the four cases, the artist’s own uploads had the largest visible results. bbno$ was the most systematic: a Short most days, almost all using his own songs and linking to the full track.
Network placements Copies, versions and remixes, with much smaller results on recent placements (bbno$: 6.1K typical vs 2.1M on his own channel, same age window). The large network totals belong to 2014–18 uploads.
Not visible publicly What each deal includes, paid or label marketing, traffic between channels, and whether network views add listening or move it. Four of the twelve (Sevdaliza, Omarion, Durand Bernarr, Lauren Sanderson) have one Nations placement or none.
What this review helps us understand

Different operating approaches are visible in the network, and they show different public results.

Channel purposes differ

Listening destinations, label catalogues and culture formats each show a distinct view pattern.

Two measures, two stories

Subscriber scale and per-upload views measure different things. Neither alone describes a channel’s value.

Large results sit near existing demand

The biggest uploads coincided with trends, known songs or versions. An association, not a cause.

Repertoire moves inside the group

Monstercat releases reappear on Trap Nation within weeks; broke.’s own channel drew more than the Nations copies of its releases.

Features follow channel type

Label channels link Shorts to releases; curators organise playlists by occasion.

The limits are real

Returning viewers, traffic sources, earnings from older uploads and economics are not visible publicly.

Public YouTube data read on 14 October 2026, plus dated third-party channel totals from February 2025. An exploratory review, not a strategy recommendation.

Three datasets

1. Full public inventory
Every public upload on the 23 channels in the four groupings reviewed: 39,215 uploads, each read individually (views, publish date, duration, owning channel) on 14 Oct 2026, 16:54–17:22 UTC. Used for lifetime views and concentration.
2. Three-year sample
8,687 uploads published 14 Oct 2023 – 14 Oct 2026 on 19 channels (including two outside comparison channels, 7clouds and Aureal), read at 13:26 and 15:42 UTC. Used for typical upload views, treatments, Shorts links, playlists, comments and case studies.
3. Dated totals and readings
Channel totals from February 2025 (us.youtubers.me snapshot histories) compared with About-page totals on 14 Oct 2026, plus two short re-reads. Used only for views gained over stated periods.

Featured-artist channels were read separately: each channel’s most recent 21–133 long-form uploads, all Shorts and playlists.

What this review does and does not show

It shows what is publicly visible: uploads, lifetime view counts, publish dates, Shorts links, playlists and a sample of comments. It doesn’t show returning viewers, subscriber vs non-subscriber views, traffic sources, watch time, revenue, cost or paid support. Network views are not assumed to be additional listening: they may replace listening that would otherwise happen elsewhere.

Measures and definitions

Lifetime views
All views an upload has gathered since publication, read 14 Oct 2026.
Typical upload views
The median (half above, half below) of lifetime views for uploads now 3–18 months old, i.e. published 21 Apr 2025 – 16 Jul 2026. A broad comparison window, not a precise age match.
Track upload
A long-form upload under ten minutes whose title isn’t a mix, set, session, compilation or interview. On the Nations channels this removes 0–1 uploads per channel; on the Monstercat channels, 6–11.
Views gained
Channel About-page total on 14 Oct 2026 minus a dated earlier total. Never annualised.
Middle half of uploads
The range between the 25th and 75th percentile.
Short
Listed on the channel’s Shorts tab (a ≤62-second duration check agreed almost exactly). Counted separately from track uploads.
Same-recording placement
A network upload with the same core title, a shared credited artist and the same version tag as a label upload, posted 0–60 days after it. Each was checked by hand.
Network placement (artists)
An upload on one of the 23 reviewed channels whose title credits the artist as main artist, feature or remixer. Same-name artists were excluded by hand.
Established song
The same song also has a 7clouds lyric video in the data: a proxy for demand that exists elsewhere.
Small samples. Chill Nation slowed/sped edits: 11 uploads. Marshmello typical: 4 uploads. Medians on these are descriptive only.

Corrections made during the review

Handles
@RnBNation, @HouseNation and @MonstercatUncaged are unrelated small channels. The real channels are @RBNation, @AllHouseNation and @Monstercat.
Same-recording rule
Tightened to same song and version, posted 0–60 days after the label’s upload. “Nevada” is now shown as a sequence of new treatments, not a same-recording placement.
Feed contamination
Videos from other channels that surfaced in a channel’s home feed were removed. R&B Nation’s last own upload is Sep 2024.
Name collisions
Artist joins require a credited-name match; same-name artists were excluded by hand.
