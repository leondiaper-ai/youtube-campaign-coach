Where the numbers came from.

The deck analyses all 181 videos currently public on the channel, read on 2 October 2026. Sources, method and the places the evidence is thin are below.

The matched campaign window

Comparing two album campaigns by calendar date would compare a 2024 campaign against a 2026 one and call the difference strategy. Instead both are measured on the same campaign clock: 71 days before release day to 14 days after. The window closes at +14 because that is how old Amulet is on the day of reading, so neither era is given more campaign time than the other.

What this does not control for
Views keep accruing, so the debut window's 155,622 reflects up to two years of accumulation and Amulet's 147,080 reflects at most twelve weeks. The two totals are not like-for-like. Both are reported as current totals, and the conclusion comes from the change in campaign behaviour — 46 uploads against 10 — rather than from the view comparison. No claim is made about which era finishes larger.

The middle row is the misleading one. Taken whole, the Amulet window's Shorts look more engaging than the debut's — 54.5 likes per thousand against 34.2. But a single post holds 29% of the views and 67% of the likes. Remove it and engagement is 25.5 against 34.2.

Why there is no recommendation on quantity
Falling mean reach across a five-fold volume increase does not establish that the extra volume was a mistake. Shorts plausibly do several jobs that view counts do not measure — discovery, continuity between long-form releases, personality, keeping a priority song moving — and a Short that holds an existing audience is not failing just because it does not reach a new one. Hence the question of which job each Short is doing, rather than how many there should be.

What this supports, and what it does not
It supports: live moments can create outsized discovery. It does not support "tour is the strongest content engine". One festival appearance is a single data point, and a festival audience is not the same as a headline club audience.

It also does not support generic concert clipping — and this is the most useful detail in the section. The channel published nine other Coachella Shorts across April 2026. They did 231 to 1,530 views each: Ya Ready Coachella? 1,530, Honest Man at Coachella vibesss 1,486, Spells, live from Coachella 1,391, Sexy Lemon live 1,252, Licorice at Coachella 1,093, Killer at Coachella! 1,031, Friday at Coachella 472, Killer @ Coachella! 400, Already reminiscing on Coachella 231.

So it was not "Coachella footage" that travelled — nine pieces of Coachella footage sat at or below the channel median. One specific post did 75,826. Whatever made that one different is the thing worth identifying before the North American run, and the deck says so rather than recommending volume.

Five posts is a small sample and the job categories are a reading, not a label YouTube supplies. Treat the whole Shorts section as a direction to test, not a measured effect size.

The visualiser reached 5,584 views in fourteen days. The BTS reached 727 in seventy-two. On absolute views the live video is the largest at 5,861, but it took 534 days to get there. Whichever way it is read, the visualiser is the strongest support format this channel has published and the BTS and the interview are the weakest.

Lyric videos have never been made on this channel. The three lyric matches in the catalogue are lyrics printed in the descriptions of Miss June, Blue Eyes and Honest Man, not lyric videos. So a lyric video is an untested option here, not an evidence-backed one — it is suggested because it is cheap and sits in the same lane as the format that did work, not because this channel has shown it performs.

One asset per format. These are single data points, and the visualiser also had the album-release moment behind it, which nothing else in the table did. Enough to say a second BTS is a poor first choice for the next song; not enough to rank the formats.

Germany is flagged, not used
Germany moved 1,269 → 29,038 views (+2,188%) and is not marked as an estimate. Cities named unprompted: Toronto, Denver, San Francisco, Detroit, Cincinnati, Houston, Auckland, Cambridge, Utah, London, plus Brazil, Italy, Mexico, Switzerland, France and Russia. The concentration is North American, which is the same direction the territory data points.

Radio is a visible discovery path. Named stations: BBC Radio 1, FIP (France), WTMD 89.7 (Baltimore), WXPN (Philadelphia). The pattern in the text is consistent — "Just heard this on the radio, had to come and find the video" — which means radio play sends people to YouTube search. It is an argument for making sure a song's official video is the obvious top result when a track gets airplay.

Fans name album tracks unprompted — "then let me", "big boy", "jade green", "actress", "muse", "wishing bone". This is free input to the follow-the-signal step: the audience is self-reporting favourites that are not all singles.

What this is not
These are relevance-ranked comments, which favours older and more-liked ones, so this is not a representative sample and the percentages describe the comments read, not the audience. No sentiment model was run; the themes are a reading. Comment volume is also tiny next to view counts. Use it as colour and as a source of hypotheses — it is why the deck quotes people rather than claiming a sentiment score.
