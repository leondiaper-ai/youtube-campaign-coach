Where the numbers came from.

Every figure on the deck comes from one pull of the YouTube Data API v3 against @freakslug (UCPPyyg5x0Txuic9N869_I_Q) on 2 October 2026, plus the Watcher's own channel snapshot series. Nothing is modelled, estimated or taken from a third party.

The one limit worth reading first

The API returned 181 of 181 public uploads and was not capped. Those 181 uploads sum to 1,368,348 views. The channel's lifetime view counter reads 5,826,056.

That is a gap of roughly 4.46 million views, or 76.5% of the channel's history, sitting in uploads that are no longer public. It is not an error in the pull — it is what a pruned channel looks like from the outside.

Consequence: every share quoted on the deck — "78.8% of views are long-form", "26% of all Shorts views" — is a share of the 1,368,348 that can be seen and counted today, not of the channel's whole history. Comparisons between currently-public assets are unaffected, because they are all measured on the same basis.

The matched campaign window

Comparing two album campaigns by calendar date would compare a 2024 campaign against a 2026 one and call the difference strategy. Instead both are measured on the same campaign clock: 71 days before release day to 14 days after. The window closes at +14 because that is how old Amulet is on the day of reading, so neither era is given more campaign time than the other.

What this does not control for
Views keep accruing, so the debut window's 155,622 reflects up to two years of accumulation and Amulet's 147,080 reflects at most twelve weeks. This makes the comparison conservative in the debut's favour — the Amulet figure can only rise against a near-static one. It does not support any claim about which era will finish larger, only that the newer one has already come within 5.5% while being a fraction of the age.

"Eight posts carried it"

The Amulet window earned 188,139 views across all 46 uploads. The seven long-form videos account for 147,080 of that, and one Short — GIRL, INTENTIONS. OUT NOW — accounts for a further 12,041.

159,121 ÷ 188,139 = 84.6%, rounded on the deck to 85%. The remaining 38 Shorts share 29,018 views between them.

What this is not
Five posts is a small sample and the categories above are my reading of each video, not a label YouTube supplies. The pattern is consistent and it points the same way as the long-form evidence, but it is a direction to test, not a measured effect size. It does not establish that a moment Short will reliably outperform — only that every Short this channel has had break out so far has been one.

Three things this read cannot answer

  • Where the 4.46 million missing views went. The removed uploads cannot be inspected from outside, so the channel's real long-term shape — including whether FRIDAY was always the outlier it looks like — is unknowable from public data.
  • Whether the Amulet window will finish ahead. Twelve weeks against two years is not a settled comparison. The claim is that it has closed to within 5.5%, not that it wins.
  • What any of this did to streaming. Nothing here touches DSP performance, and YouTube view totals are not a proxy for it.
