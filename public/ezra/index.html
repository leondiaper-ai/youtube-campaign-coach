Beyond Ezra

What this campaign teaches us.

01

Don’t go quiet.

Consistency gave the campaign a continuous presence. A 43-day silence last time became a 9-day maximum gap this time — a decision, not a budget.

02

Don’t bet everything on one video.

When 63% of a campaign sits on a single upload, the campaign is that upload. Build multiple ways in instead.

03

Give Shorts and long-form different jobs.

Shorts keep the channel present and catch the moments. Long-form gives people somewhere deeper to go. Judge each on its own job.

04

Build a world, not a content calendar.

Different formats can still feel like one campaign. Three films for the album’s three movements is what turns sixty-nine uploads into one thing.

Methodology notes
Data sources
Public YouTube Data API, our weekly Watcher snapshots of that API, and public comments. No YouTube Studio metrics were used.
Comparison method
Comparable announcement-led campaign windows — 101 days announcement-to-album in 2024, 107 in 2026. Structural measures are used where asset age prevents a fair absolute-view comparison, so campaign view totals are not compared.
Public subscribers
Displayed counts are rounded by YouTube, so movement is shown as observed readings rather than as a precise figure or a growth multiple.
Attribution
Public data shows co-occurrence, not asset-level causation. Release-week channel movement is not credited to any individual upload, and audience comments are not presented as a cause of any view figure.
Comments
Used qualitatively, from a public relevance-ranked sample rather than a census.
Pre-campaign material
The Alexandra Palace film predates the campaign window. It is used only as an audience signal; its views are excluded from every campaign-performance figure in the deck.
Catalogue reconciliation
Retrieved public uploads do not fully reconcile with the channel’s reported lifetime view total, so no share-of-lifetime claims are made.
