The question everybody asks

Is this best in class?

Not yet.

What we can say

  • Sixteen of sixteen weeks active, with a nine-day maximum gap
  • 2.6× the output of the comparable 2024 campaign
  • Far less dependence on a single video — 84.7% → 56.5%
  • A fully stocked channel around release
  • A clear public channel movement in release week

What we can't say yet

  • That the outcome is best in class — release week is one observation, and it's a week old
  • That the lift becomes sustained growth — too early to know
  • Which asset caused anything — no traffic-source data exists
  • That it beats anyone but Ezra. Best in class needs a class, and so far this is Ezra measured against Ezra

The structure is strong. The outcome needs time.

The gap-length and week-coverage distribution across the wider Watcher roster has not been computed. Until it is, "a marked improvement on their own previous campaign" is the claim the evidence supports — and that is a real one.
Beyond Ezra

What this campaign teaches us.

01

Don't go quiet.

A 43-day silence in the last campaign became a 9-day maximum gap in this one. Continuous presence is a decision, and it is one you can make without a bigger budget.

02

Don't bet it all
on one video.

When 63% of a campaign sits on a single upload, the campaign is that upload. Spreading attention across more assets and more formats is what changed here.

03

Build depth
around the music.

Official videos joined by rehearsal films, live footage, visualisers, spoken pieces and Shorts. When release week came, there was somewhere for everyone to go.

Analysis · method, limits and audit trail

What we can and cannot see

This deck is built entirely on public data: the YouTube Data API v3, and our own weekly Watcher snapshots of that same public API. There are no YouTube Studio metrics anywhere in it. No impressions, no click-through rate, no retention, no traffic sources, no unique viewers, no new-versus-returning. We do not have authenticated access to this channel and none of those numbers has been estimated.

The hole in the data, disclosed

The 251 retrieved rows sum to 12,223,598 views. The channel reports 27,305,501. A gap of 15,081,903 views — 55.2% of the reported lifetime total — is not accounted for by the public uploads playlist. Deleted or privated uploads are the most likely explanation; we have not diagnosed it. Consequence: this deck makes no share-of-lifetime claims at all. Every percentage on every slide uses a campaign window as its denominator, never the lifetime total.

Why no view comparison between the two campaigns

The 2024 assets have had roughly two years to accumulate views. The 2026 assets have had between three days and sixteen weeks. Comparing the two windows' view totals would not be a measurement, it would be an artefact of age — and we cannot reconstruct what 2024 looked like at day 111, because the API returns only current view counts and our weekly snapshots do not reach back that far.

What survives the age problem, and is therefore what the deck uses: upload counts, active weeks, gap lengths, format mix, and any ratio computed inside a single window. What does not survive, and is therefore absent: every absolute view comparison, every "bigger than last time", and the median-views comparison.

The release date is unresolved

The album's announcement Short, published 28 May, reads "Out September 18th 2026". Everything else on the channel points to 11 September: a Short on 10 September saying "out tomorrow", the visualisers landing across 10–11 September, and the block party Short on 12 September. We have not confirmed the commercial release date with Partisan. Because the framing of the release-week comparison depends on it, this deck states no precise date and uses "release week" throughout. This should be resolved before the deck goes further.

Subscriber wording, and why there is no multiple

Every subscriber figure here is the publicly displayed count, which YouTube rounds — to the nearest 100 at this channel size — and which already nets off unsubscribes. It is not gross or net subscriber additions; those are Studio metrics we do not have. The weekly movements read 300, 400, 300, then 1,100. Because each of those is rounded to the nearest hundred, a ratio built from them could honestly be anything from about 2.6× to 4.4×, so the deck states no subscriber multiple at all and shows the four readings instead. The weekly view figures are not rounded, which is why 2.4× is stated for those.

Two limits on this table. First, our weekly snapshots only begin on 24 August, so the before-and-after evidence is strong for release week specifically and does not exist for the campaign as a whole. Second, the 14 September snapshot and the 15 September API read return an identical view total, which suggests they share a source rather than being two independent observations a day apart.

Two numbers that are not the same quantity

The 19 release-week uploads have accumulated 242,582 views between them as of 15 September. The channel recorded 535,544 views in the seven days to 14 September. These measure different things — the first is the lifetime-to-date total of a set of videos, the second is channel-wide viewing across the whole catalogue in a fixed period — and the deck never places them side by side, because doing so invites a comparison that isn't valid.

Attribution: what we are not claiming

We have no traffic-source data, so we cannot say whether release-week movement came from the visualisers, the Rehearsal Tapes, the block party, press, playlisting, the tour announcement, or spillover from streaming release radars. Slide 7 and slide 8 describe events that co-occurred. Nothing in this deck credits an asset with an outcome.

The Alexandra Palace film

Pre-campaign The live film with Kano was published 8 May 2026, nineteen days before the album announcement. Its 337,985 views are excluded from every campaign figure in this deck. It appears on slide 6 solely as evidence about how this audience responds to long-form film, labelled as pre-campaign, and its views are never shown as campaign performance.

The comment evidence, and what it will not carry

The comment scan returns a relevance-ranked sample of up to 40 comments per video, not a census. Alexandra Palace has 251 comments in total and we retrieved 40. Every quotation is drawn from the comments YouTube surfaces most prominently, which is not the same as what the commenter base as a whole is saying.

An earlier draft of the analysis claimed this audience was "actively hunting" for long-form live footage, on the strength of two comments describing searching for a full set. That claim was cut: two comments on one out-of-window video cannot carry it. The narrower claim on slide 6 — that when Ezra publish a well-made long-form film, the comments are about how it was made — is what the evidence actually supports.

Classification

Shorts versus long-form uses the API's own isShort flag. Format labels (music video, visualiser, rehearsal film, live, Shorts) are derived from titles and durations. The 2026 window opens at the album announcement Short of 27 May and closes at the pull date. The 2024 window opens at its lead single and runs 110 days, chosen to sit within one day of the 2026 window so that structural comparison is clean.

Where the design came from

The palette was sampled programmatically from fifteen real campaign thumbnails — greys and crushed blacks filtered out, the rest binned by hue. The warm band came back at 48% of saturated pixels, green at 17%, red at 11%, teal at 12%: the Lagos gele and foliage, the brass in Only Love, the Kingston sequins, the sea in Well Organised. The repeating rhythm strip is one tick per day of the campaign window, lit where an upload landed — the deck's central finding used as its decoration.
