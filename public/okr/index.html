Three things we could measure together

Better visibility helps teams engage, deep dives turn information into ideas, and territory data helps focus attention. Here’s what that could look like as results.

Team engagement

More teams working from shared YouTube information and bringing it into their own conversations.

Consistent artist activity

Priority artists active in more weeks, with shorter gaps and more follow-up around releases.

Views and consumption

Release weeks against the weeks before, and territory consumption over comparable periods.

Targets and ownership are the group’s to decide. Three questions to start the conversation:

Where could this be useful?Which measures would help?What would teams need to take this further?

Three lenses, read separately and never combined into a score: what our pipe reports for the UK, what Chartmetric estimates for UK audience, and the global channel figures. D-Block was the one artist all three agreed on, which is what made it the first to get a closer look.

Rankings are scoped to the dataset reviewed. “#2” is within the 145 artist strings in our UK consumption data for the period, and “#1” is within the crossover and landscape samples given Chartmetric territory reads. The slide itself shows the two figures rather than the ranks. Neither is a claim about UK rap or about Virgin’s roster as a whole: the Virgin UK universe reviewed has only three rap artists, so a “#1 Virgin artist” line was removed from the D-Block deck for exactly that reason.
What the consumption data is and isn’t. VMG UK’s own YouTube reporting (Daily Trend, YouTube tab) for repertoire through our pipe: 51.0M views across 145 artist strings in the period. It is period-level, carries no Shorts/long-form, art-track or UGC split, and is not every UK YouTube view Virgin generates. About half the consumption strings are not yet matched to a tracked channel, and some Virgin UK artists with real UK audiences (Peter Gabriel, Bloc Party, Ezra Collective, IDLES among them) have no record in it at all — which is why the slide says local knowledge has to sit beside the data.
Status. The D-Block review (/dblock) was built for the management conversation. Its recommendations are options; none is recorded as implemented, and no result is claimed. The 474M gap between the channel’s lifetime counter and the sum of its public uploads is unresolved and no share-of-lifetime claim depends on it.

The measures — what could be read today

Team engagement
Readable now: teams with live boards and artists they have added; reviews requested and delivered. Not a proxy: a workspace existing.
Consistent artist activity
Readable now from public upload dates: active weeks and longest gap per campaign window, for any tracked channel; and whether a priority release is followed by a long-form moment within an agreed window (the window itself is a choice for the group, not a rule).
Views and consumption
Readable now: release-week channel views against the preceding weeks (Watcher weekly snapshots, from late August 2026); UK consumption per artist from our own reporting, period-level. With authorised Studio access in aggregate: traffic sources, returning viewers and the catalogue-to-new-release question, which public data cannot answer.
