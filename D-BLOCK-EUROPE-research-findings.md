# D-BLOCK EUROPE — RESEARCH FINDINGS

**Internal. Research layer for the deck rebuild. Not the deck.**
15 September 2026 · full public catalogue (197/197) · 15-channel UK rap peer set · comment samples · external YouTube surfaces

---

## THE ONE-LINE VERSION

**D-Block Europe built a world-class launch system and never built a response system.**

65 of their 66 visualisers were made on four album-release days. The channel has exactly one gear, and it is an excellent one. What it has never had is a way to react to a song that takes off *after* release — which is why 11 of their 15 biggest videos, carrying **249.9m views between them, half the entire catalogue**, lead to nothing at all.

That is the strategic finding. Everything below either supports it, corrects the brief, or arms it.

## PART 1 — THE TWELVE DISCOVERIES

Ranked by how hard they'll land in the room.

### 1. Eleven of the fifteen biggest videos are dead ends — 249.9m views, 50.7% of the catalogue
For each of DBE's 15 biggest videos I looked for any other asset for the same song, ever. Eleven have none.

**Overseas — 106,070,561 views. No second asset. And the channel published nothing at all for the next 84 days.** `[API]`

Also dead: Elegant & Gang (22.7m), Make You Smile (19.3m), Kevin McCallister (18.4m), Birds Are Chirping (16.6m), Bankroll Got Bigger (13.5m), Outside (13.1m), 3 Goddy Remix (11.6m), BIG B (10.4m), No Competition (9.9m), Man In The Mirror (8.3m).

Only four of the fifteen had a second door: Ferrari Horses, Pakistan, Eagle, Press Da Button.

> **Slide: "106 MILLION PEOPLE CAME THROUGH THIS DOOR."** Then: 84 days of silence behind it. This is the strongest single slide available.

### 2. 65 of 66 visualisers came from four days
The visualiser library looks like a programming strategy. It isn't — it's a release-day ritual. 65 of 66 were uploaded on one of four album-drop days (7 Nov 2019, 10 Jul 2023, 12 Jan 2024, 14 Nov 2025). `[API]`

**DBE has never once made a visualiser because a song was working.** That's the missing mechanism, stated as a fact rather than an opinion. It also explains #1 — the machine only fires on release day, so anything that catches fire later gets nothing.

### 3. DBE is the most consistently present channel in UK rap — #1 of 15
This overturns the assumption the earlier deck was built on. Across a 15-channel UK rap peer set: `[PEER]`

| | DBE | Next best | Worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active months, last 24 | **15/24 — #1** | Aitch / Clavish 13 | Dave, Stormzy 3 |
| Uploads, last 12 months | **45 — #1** | Nines 39 | Stormzy 1 |
| Longest silence, 24 months | **124 days — #2** | Clavish 115 | Nines 616 |

Central Cee has been dark **170 days**. Dave **201**. Stormzy is functionally dormant.

> The "DBE goes quiet between albums" story is **false**, and we should say so loudly and early. It's the celebration slide that's also true.

### 4. Their real weakness is burstiness, not silence
45 uploads across only **22 active days** in 24 months — 2.0 uploads per active day, vs Clavish's 1.07. Median gap between active days: **27 days**, second-worst among the genuinely active channels. `[PEER]`

Clavish achieves better silence-avoidance than DBE **with zero bulk drops in 24 months**. The fix is distribution, not volume. This pairs with #2: the bursts *are* the album days.

### 7. Do NOT build live content — the evidence is brutal and it's about DBE specifically
The brief's "DBE TV" thesis assumed a live pillar. The data kills it. `[PEER]` `[OBSERVED]`

- Across the peer set, self-produced live returns **0.1%–2.3%** of catalogue views. Clavish shot an entire *Live From London* series for **0.2%** of his views.

## PART 3 — WHAT "ALWAYS ON" HONESTLY BECOMES

The idea survives; the broadcast framing doesn't. Three pillars the inventory supports, one it doesn't:

1. **The catalogue engine** — fully supported, zero production cost. 197 videos, 66 visualisers, a 106m flagship, 270m more sitting on GRM. Organising and surfacing this needs no camera.
2. **Personality, repatriated not invented** — strongly supported. The vlogs already work at 124k–557k an episode and they're dormant on Adz's own channel. This is a rights-and-relationship conversation, not a production budget.
3. **The archive** — contingent. Adz says the footage exists and a documentary is in the works. Verify before planning around it.
4. **Live** — cut it.

**Better framing than "DBE TV":** the channel has one gear and needs a second. Call it what it is — **a way to respond when a song takes off.** Rich Low Life is trending right now with no video, and the current system has no mechanism to serve it. That's the proposition, and it's concrete rather than aspirational.

## PART 5 — WHAT I COULD NOT ESTABLISH

- **Current view velocity.** A single API snapshot gives cumulative lifetime views only. Every "still active" claim in this document is a **lifetime daily average**, which is exact but is not proof of current activity. Do not let anyone phrase it as "is doing 60k/day today."
- **Shorts sound usage / TikTok.** Not observable with these tools — and it's the gap most likely to contradict the Worldwide Wave read, since DBE's audience skews short-form. The four-track recommendation is provisional until someone checks TikTok sound creation counts.
- **Whether the documentary footage exists.** Artist claim, four days old, unverified.
- **Collaboration retroactivity.** Still unverified in Studio. Two recommendations depend on it.
- **No duo or group in the peer set.** DBE is the only one. The personality finding in particular is untested against a like-for-like structure — Krept & Konan or NSG would be the comparators.
- **Worldwide Wave chart position.** Absent from the Official Albums Chart Top 100 in release week. Anomalous for an act with nine Top 10 albums. Cause not verified — possibly a mixtape-vs-album registration question. Do not assert either reading.
- **Comment data is relevance-ranked and capped.** Raw counts only, never proportions. Some bot contamination observed ("AUTHENTIC VIEWS" spam ranking into top comments).
